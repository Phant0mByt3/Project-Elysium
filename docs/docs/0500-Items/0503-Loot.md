# 0503 — Loot

## 1. Overview

General loot design principles governing how items drop, are distributed, and feel to receive. See [0504-Loot-Tables.md](0504-Loot-Tables.md) for the specific per-encounter tables built on top of these principles.

## 2. Rarity Tiers

* **Common (White)** — vendor trash, sold for Aurum.
* **Uncommon (Green)** — basic questing/leveling gear.
* **Rare (Blue)** — dungeon-quality gear, meaningful upgrades.
* **Epic (Purple)** — raid-quality gear and top-tier crafted items.
* **Legendary (Orange)** — unique, named items with distinct effects; see [0505-Legendary-Items.md](0505-Legendary-Items.md).
* **Relic (Gold)** — the rarest tier, tied directly to Age of Concord artifacts; see [0506-Relics.md](0506-Relics.md).

Full rarity design rules live in [0510-Item-Rarity.md](0510-Item-Rarity.md).

## 3. Distribution Rules

* Group loot uses a need/greed/pass roll system for group-eligible drops, with personal loot as the default for solo-lootable trash and quest rewards.
* Loot should be visible and celebratory — a rare-or-higher drop should always have a distinct visual/audio cue.
* Bosses should never feel like a "loot pinata" — see [0504-Loot-Tables.md](0504-Loot-Tables.md) for drop rate philosophy.

## 4. Player Fantasy

Receiving loot should feel like a story beat, not just a numbers update — item flavor text (see [1403-Quest-Writing-Guide.md](../1400-Development/1403-Quest-Writing-Guide.md) tone standards) should tie meaningfully into the lore of where it dropped.

## 5. Loot Pacing

Leveling content is tuned to provide a steady drip of Uncommon and occasional Rare upgrades, avoiding both loot droughts that make questing feel unrewarding and loot floods that trivialize the itemization curve. Endgame content follows a steeper, more deliberate pacing curve tied to weekly lockouts (see [0107-Raids.md](../0100-World/0107-Raids.md)).

## 6. Bad Luck Protection

To avoid the frustration of extreme drop variance, high-value loot pools (Epic-and-above item slots in raid content) use a bad-luck-protection mechanism that increases individual drop chance the longer a player goes without receiving an eligible item from that specific pool.

## 7. Loot and the Economy

Loot that a player doesn't need feeds directly into the economy through disenchanting (see [0509-Enchanting.md](0509-Enchanting.md)), selling to vendors, or listing on the Auction House ([1003-Auction-House.md](../1000-Economy/1003-Auction-House.md)), making loot design and economy design tightly interlinked.
