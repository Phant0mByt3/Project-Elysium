# 1. Overview

Religion in Elysium is defined by one fact above all others:

**The gods are silent.**

Since the Sundering, none of the Elysian Circle have spoken directly to mortals.

There have been no confirmed divine appearances.

No god has openly descended upon a city.

No prophet has received an undeniable message from the heavens.

Yet the gods remain deeply embedded in Elysian culture.

Their names survive in prayers.

Their symbols remain carved into ancient temples.

Their stories are passed between generations.

Their followers continue to build shrines, observe rituals, and interpret the events of the world through the teachings left behind before the Sundering.

Some believe the gods are still watching.

Some believe they are dead.

Some believe they abandoned Elysium.

Others believe the gods are waiting for mortals to prove that they can survive without them.

No mortal knows the truth.

This uncertainty is central to religion in Elysium.

---

# 2. Faith After the Sundering

The Sundering fundamentally changed religion.

Before the Sundering, divine presence was believed to be much more direct.

Temples were larger.

Religious institutions held significant political influence.

Miracles were recorded regularly.

The gods were considered active participants in mortal civilization.

After the Sundering, that certainty disappeared.

Entire temples were destroyed.

Priests lost contact with their religious institutions.

Holy texts were lost.

Different communities began interpreting the same gods in different ways.

Over the following centuries, these differences became distinct religious traditions.

Modern Elysian religions are therefore not always exact representations of what the gods originally taught.

They are mortal interpretations of incomplete history.

---

# 3. The Elysian Circle

The seven surviving gods of the Elysian Circle remain the foundation of most major religions.

Each god is associated with particular concepts, cultures, and traditions.

However, no god belongs exclusively to one race or faction.

A god may be worshipped differently by different cultures.

The same deity can represent completely different ideas depending on who is interpreting them.

---

# 4. The Solar Path

**God:** Solthar
**Domains:** Light, Sun
**Major Regions:** Aurelia
**Strongest Following:** Humans and Dawnbound communities
**Primary Temple:** Sunspire Cathedral, Solmere

The Solar Path is one of the largest religious traditions in modern Elysium.

It teaches that civilization survives through cooperation, responsibility, and the willingness to rebuild after destruction.

The sun represents both literal light and the idea that darkness should never be accepted as permanent.

The faith became closely associated with the Dawnbound Concord because both movements emerged from the desire to restore civilization after the Age of Ash.

However, Solthar's followers do not universally support the Concord.

Some believe the Dawnbound have become too obsessed with recreating the past.

Others believe Solthar's teachings demand that civilization move forward rather than simply restore what was lost.

### Common Teachings

* Protect those who cannot protect themselves.
* Preserve knowledge.
* Rebuild what can be rebuilt.
* Do not allow fear to become an excuse for cruelty.
* Remember the mistakes of the past.

### Common Symbol

A rising sun surrounded by seven rays.

### Common Ritual

The First Light.

Followers gather before sunrise and remain silent until the first light appears.

---

# 5. The Night Vigil

**God:** Nyxara
**Domains:** Night, Moon, Shadow, Secrets
**Major Regions:** Urban centres and Duskward communities
**Strongest Following:** Rogues, spies, scouts, and independent communities

The Night Vigil teaches that darkness is not inherently evil.

Night represents uncertainty, privacy, and the parts of the world that cannot be understood immediately.

Its followers often believe the Age of Concord failed because mortals became too confident in their understanding of the world.

The Night Vigil therefore values:

* Independence
* Caution
* Adaptability
* Secrets
* Personal responsibility

The faith became associated with the Duskward Pact because of its rejection of blind nostalgia.

Still, the Night Vigil is not an inherently Duskward religion.

Many people who simply value privacy or independence follow Nyxara without having any political allegiance.

### Common Teachings

* Not every truth must be revealed.
* Survival sometimes requires adaptation.
* Trust should be earned.
* Fear of the unknown should not control you.
* The past should inform the future, not command it.

### Common Symbol

A crescent moon partially covered by a dark circle.

### Common Ritual

The Night Watch.

Followers spend part of the night in silence, reflecting on things they do not yet understand.

---

# 6. The Stone Covenant

**God:** Terravox
**Domains:** Earth, Stone, Endurance
**Major Regions:** Vethmoor
**Strongest Following:** Dwarves

The Stone Covenant is central to dwarven society.

Terravox represents the permanence of stone and the endurance required to survive beneath the mountains.

Dwarven religious tradition treats craftsmanship as a form of devotion.

A well-made sword, bridge, fortress, or machine can be considered an act of worship.

The faith also places enormous importance on ancestry.

The dead are remembered through the structures they built and the knowledge they left behind.

### Common Teachings

* Build things that outlast you.
* Keep your promises.
* Protect the clan.
* Respect those who came before you.
* Do not abandon something simply because it is difficult.

### Common Symbol

A mountain divided by a vertical line representing a forged pillar.

### Common Ritual

The Keeping of the Stone.

A newly completed structure receives a ceremonial stone engraved with the names of those who built it.

---

# 7. The Forge Flame

**God:** Ignareth
**Domains:** Fire, Forge, Creation, Conflict
**Major Regions:** Vethmoor
**Strongest Following:** Orcs and dwarven smiths

The Forge Flame is unusual because it is openly respected by cultures on both sides of the faction divide.

Dwarves traditionally associate Ignareth with craftsmanship.

Orcs traditionally associate him with fire, strength, warfare, and the transformation of raw materials into something stronger.

The two traditions are very different.

Dwarven worship tends to emphasize creation.

Orcish worship tends to emphasize transformation.

Both nevertheless recognize the same fundamental principle:

**Nothing becomes stronger without passing through fire.**

### Common Teachings

* Strength must be tested.
* Creation requires effort.
* Failure is part of improvement.
* Power without discipline destroys its wielder.
* What survives the forge is stronger for having endured it.

### Common Symbol

A flame surrounding a hammer.

### Common Ritual

The Kindling.

A community lights a single central fire during important ceremonies.

The flame is kept alive throughout the event.

---

# 8. The Green Communion

**God:** Aeloria
**Domains:** Life, Nature, Growth
**Major Regions:** Forests, rural settlements, Wildwood Reach
**Strongest Following:** Druids, rangers, farmers, herbalists, and healers

The Green Communion teaches that life is a cycle rather than a straight path.

Growth, decay, death, and renewal are all considered natural parts of existence.

The faith strongly rejects the idea that nature exists purely for mortal exploitation.

At the same time, most followers do not oppose civilization.

A farm, village, or city can exist alongside nature if it respects the land that sustains it.

Aeloria's silence is particularly painful to her followers.

Many druids spent centuries believing that nature itself would eventually provide an answer.

It never did.

This has produced several interpretations.

Some believe Aeloria is dead.

Some believe she withdrew from the mortal world.

Some believe nature itself is her answer.

Others believe that her silence means mortals are finally expected to act without divine guidance.

### Common Teachings

* Life should be protected.
* Death is part of nature.
* Growth requires change.
* Take only what is needed.
* Leave something behind for those who come after you.

### Common Symbol

A leaf growing around a circular seed.

### Common Ritual

The Returning.

Followers plant a seed in memory of someone who has died.

---

# 9. The Quiet Road

**God:** Voranthe
**Domains:** Death, Fate, Passage
**Major Regions:** Revenant communities and northern settlements
**Strongest Following:** Revenants, undertakers, historians, and some healers

The Quiet Road is often misunderstood by outsiders.

It is not a death cult.

Its followers do not worship death itself.

Instead, they believe death is one of the unavoidable transitions of existence.

The faith places great importance on accepting mortality.

This became especially significant after the appearance of the Revenants.

Many Revenants found themselves caught between the traditional concepts of life and death.

The Quiet Road became one of the few religious traditions willing to accept them without demanding that they explain what they had become.

### Common Teachings

* Everything has an ending.
* Do not fear what cannot be avoided.
* Remember the dead.
* Do not allow grief to erase the life that came before it.
* Fate may guide a path, but mortals still choose how to walk it.

### Common Symbol

A road disappearing into a dark horizon.

### Common Ritual

The Last Step.

When someone dies, their name is spoken once by the community and then followed by a period of silence.

---

# 10. The Storm Call

**God:** Maelithir
**Domains:** Sea, Storm, Change
**Major Regions:** Coastal Aurelia and maritime settlements
**Strongest Following:** Sailors, fishermen, explorers, and coastal communities

The Storm Call is built around the idea that the sea cannot be controlled.

The ocean represents uncertainty.

Storms represent change.

Calm seas represent temporary peace rather than permanent safety.

Followers of Maelithir often believe that trying to eliminate uncertainty is impossible.

Instead, mortals should learn to survive it.

The faith became particularly important during the Age of Reclamation as sailors began crossing waters that had been inaccessible for centuries.

Some followers believe the changing seas are a sign that Maelithir is returning.

Others believe the storms are simply natural consequences of the world's recovery.

### Common Teachings

* Respect forces greater than yourself.
* Do not mistake calm for safety.
* Change is unavoidable.
* Preparation is more valuable than certainty.
* The unknown should be explored, not feared.

### Common Symbol

A wave surrounding a single lightning bolt.

### Common Ritual

The First Voyage.

Sailors traditionally pour a small amount of water into the sea before beginning a major journey.

---

# 11. Kaelgorath and the Broken Faiths

**God:** Kaelgorath
**Domains:** Originally unknown or disputed
**Current Associations:** The Sundering, destruction, corruption

Kaelgorath was once associated with the Elysian Circle.

His modern religious identity is complicated.

Most major religions reject him entirely.

Others believe that the surviving records of his teachings were deliberately destroyed.

The Ashen Circle studies his history rather than worshipping him.

However, scattered cults have emerged throughout the centuries.

These groups are collectively referred to as the **Broken Faiths**.

They are not one organized religion.

Different groups interpret Kaelgorath differently.

Some believe he was betrayed by the other gods.

Some believe the Sundering was necessary.

Some believe mortal civilization should be destroyed again.

Others simply worship the power associated with his remaining fragments.

The Ashen Circle considers these groups dangerous because they may unknowingly strengthen Kaelgorath's influence.

# 12. Religious Diversity

Religion does not determine faction allegiance.

A person's faith and political allegiance are separate parts of their identity.

Examples include:

```text
Human
Dawnbound
Follower of Nyxara
```

```text
Orc
Duskward
Follower of Aeloria
```

```text
Dwarf
Dawnbound
Follower of Ignareth
```

```text
Revenant
Duskward
Follower of Solthar
```

These combinations should be completely valid.

This prevents religion from becoming a simple extension of the faction system.

---

# 13. Religious Conflict

Religious disagreements should exist throughout Elysium.

Different interpretations may conflict over:

* The meaning of the Sundering
* Whether the gods are alive
* Whether the gods should return
* The morality of resurrection
* The treatment of Revenants
* The use of ancient magic
* Whether civilization should be restored
* Whether nature should be protected from expansion
* Whether Kaelgorath can truly be destroyed

These disagreements create story opportunities without requiring one religion to be objectively correct.

---

# 14. Religious Institutions

Religious organizations vary considerably.

Some are centralized.

Others are local.

Examples include:

### Temples

Permanent religious buildings found in cities and settlements.

### Shrines

Small places of worship found throughout the world.

### Orders

Groups dedicated to a particular religious purpose.

### Monasteries

Communities focused on study, discipline, and spiritual practice.

### Pilgrimages

Journeys to important religious locations.

### Household Faith

Many ordinary citizens worship privately without belonging to an organized institution.

---

# 15. Temples and Shrines

Temples should be important cultural locations rather than mandatory gameplay hubs.

They can provide:

* Lore
* Optional quests
* Dialogue
* Festivals
* Historical information
* Cosmetic rewards
* Religious items
* Environmental storytelling

Some ancient temples may also contain hidden areas or secrets.

A ruined shrine might contain an inscription that reveals information about the Age of Concord.

---

# 16. Religion and Classes

Class identity can draw inspiration from religious traditions without requiring religious allegiance.

For example:

### Paladin

May be associated with Solthar, but can follow any compatible faith.

### Mage

May study religious magic or ancient divine traditions without worshipping a god.

### Druid

Often associated with Aeloria, but may follow other interpretations of nature.

### Ranger

May respect Aeloria, Maelithir, or no god at all.

### Warrior

May follow Ignareth, Terravox, Solthar, or another tradition.

### Rogue

May follow Nyxara or simply reject organized religion.

Classes should never automatically determine a player's religion.

---

# 17. Divine Magic

The existence of divine magic does not prove that the gods are actively communicating with mortals.

Different cultures have different explanations for supernatural abilities.

Possible explanations include:

* The gods still grant power indirectly.
* Ancient divine energy remains in the world.
* Mortals learned to manipulate divine forces.
* The gods' influence survives through relics.
* Faith itself creates supernatural power.
* Nobody actually understands where the power comes from.

The game should deliberately avoid confirming one explanation too early.

This mystery can become important to future storylines.

---

# 18. Religious Relics

Ancient religious relics can survive from the Age of Concord.

Examples include:

* Broken holy weapons
* Temple seals
* Sacred stones
* Ancient books
* Ceremonial masks
* Divine symbols
* Lost banners
* Fragmented statues

Relics should not automatically be magical.

Some are valuable simply because of their historical importance.

---

# 19. Festivals

Religious festivals are part of Elysian culture.

Different regions may celebrate different traditions.

Examples include:

### First Light

A Solthar festival celebrating renewal.

### Night of Silence

A Nyxara observance focused on reflection.

### Stonebinding

A Terravox celebration associated with craftsmanship.

### The Great Kindling

An Ignareth festival centered around communal fire.

### Returning Spring

An Aelorian celebration of growth.

### The Quiet Passing

A Voranthe remembrance ceremony.

### Stormwake

A Maelithir festival marking the beginning of the sailing season.

Religious festivals should appear in the seasonal event system where appropriate.

---

# 20. Religion and Dialogue

NPCs should speak about religion according to their own beliefs.

A priest should not automatically know the true nature of the gods.

A historian may disagree with a priest.

A soldier may not care about religion at all.

A Revenant may have complicated feelings about Voranthe.

A dwarf may treat Ignareth's teachings as part of everyday life rather than formal religion.

This keeps religion grounded in character rather than turning every NPC into a lore source.

---

# 21. Player Religious Identity

Players may optionally select a personal faith.

This should primarily affect:

* Dialogue
* Cosmetic titles
* Religious quests
* Shrine interactions
* Lore
* Festivals
* Optional rewards

It should not permanently lock the player into a religion.

Players should be able to change their beliefs through gameplay if the system eventually supports it.

---

# 22. Religious Reputation

Some religious organizations may have their own reputation systems.

For example:

```text
Solar Path
Neutral
```

or:

```text
Ashen Circle
Friendly
```

Religious reputation can unlock:

* Titles
* Cosmetic items
* Lore
* Special dialogue
* Optional quests
* Religious cosmetics
* Shrine interactions

It should not replace faction reputation.

---

# 23. The Central Religious Question

The greatest religious question in modern Elysium is not:

**"Which god is real?"**

The gods are known to have existed.

The greater question is:

**"Why are they silent?"**

Some believe they are dead.

Some believe they are imprisoned.

Some believe they abandoned Elysium.

Some believe they are waiting.

Some believe the Sundering changed the gods themselves.

Some believe the gods are speaking through the world and mortals simply no longer know how to listen.

The truth should remain a major mystery within Elysium's long-term lore.

---

# 24. Design Rules

1. Religion should enrich Elysium rather than control it.
2. No faction should own a religion exclusively.
3. No race should be forced to worship a specific god.
4. Class does not determine religion.
5. Religion does not determine faction.
6. The gods remain silent unless the main story explicitly establishes otherwise.
7. Religious NPCs should have personal interpretations rather than universal knowledge.
8. Different cultures should interpret the same god differently.
9. Religious conflict should not always have a clear winner.
10. Religion should never block the main story.
11. Religious choices should primarily affect optional content.
12. Ancient religious traditions should contribute to environmental storytelling.
13. Kaelgorath's followers should remain distinct from legitimate worship of the Elysian Circle.
14. Divine magic should remain mysterious.
15. New religions should only be introduced when they represent a meaningful cultural or historical development.
16. Existing religions should evolve as Elysium's story progresses.

---

# 25. Core Principle

Religion in Elysium is not about knowing which god is right.

It is about what mortals believe in a world where the beings who created it have gone silent.

The Solar Path sees the silence as a test.

The Night Vigil sees it as proof that mortals must rely on themselves.

The Stone Covenant sees it as a reason to endure.

The Forge Flame sees it as another challenge to survive.

The Green Communion sees it as a wound in the natural order.

The Quiet Road accepts it as another transition.

The Storm Call sees it as a reminder that the world cannot be controlled.

And somewhere in the ruins of the old world, there are people who believe Kaelgorath's silence means something entirely different.

No living mortal knows which interpretation is correct.

That uncertainty is part of Elysium.
