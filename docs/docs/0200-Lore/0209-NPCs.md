# 0209 — NPCs

Named characters throughout Elysium.

NPCs are one of the primary ways players experience the history, cultures, factions, kingdoms, religions, and conflicts of the world.

A named NPC should feel like an actual person rather than a quest marker.

Every major NPC should have a reason to exist beyond providing gameplay functionality.

---

# NPC Categories

## Quest Givers

NPCs who provide quests.

Every quest-giving NPC must have:

* A name
* A home location
* A role
* A reason for giving the quest
* At least one line of flavor dialogue outside the quest text
* A connection to the surrounding world

See [1404-NPC-Writing-Guide.md](../1400-Development/1404-NPC-Writing-Guide.md).

---

## Merchants & Trainers

Functional NPCs who provide gameplay services.

Examples include:

* General merchants
* Weapon merchants
* Armor merchants
* Class trainers
* Profession trainers
* Blacksmiths
* Alchemists
* Enchanters
* Stablemasters
* Innkeepers
* Bankers

Even functional NPCs should have names and basic personalities.

A blacksmith should feel like a blacksmith who happens to sell equipment, not a shop interface with a humanoid model.

---

## Rulers & Leaders

Named political figures associated with:

* Kingdoms
* Regional factions
* Major factions
* Clans
* Councils
* Religious organizations
* Military organizations

They should generally appear through quests, world events, political storylines, or important locations rather than functioning as ordinary vendors.

See [0203-Factions.md](../0200-Lore/0203-Factions.md) and [0205-Kingdoms.md](../0200-Lore/0205-Kingdoms.md).

---

## Companions

Recurring NPCs who travel with or repeatedly assist the player.

Companions should have their own motivations and character arcs.

They should not exist purely to explain lore to the player.

Companions are particularly important in side stories.

See [0208-Side-Stories.md](../0200-Lore/0208-Side-Stories.md).

---

## Named Enemies

Unique hostile NPCs with names, personalities, and specific mechanics.

Examples include:

* Dungeon bosses
* Raid bosses
* World bosses
* Faction commanders
* Criminal leaders
* Cult leaders
* Rival adventurers
* Regional warlords

Generic enemies do not need to be listed here unless they become individually important.

See:

# Major Launch NPCs

## High Councilor Aldwin Rae

**Role:** Ruler / Political Leader
**Faction:** Dawnbound Concord
**Kingdom:** Concord Dominion
**Location:** Solmere
**Questline:** Main Story — Act I

Aldwin Rae is the current High Councilor of the Concord Dominion.

He represents the political leadership of the Dawnbound Concord in Solmere.

Aldwin believes strongly in reconstruction and cooperation between the surviving civilizations.

He considers the Reconnection an opportunity that Elysium cannot afford to waste.

At the same time, he is cautious about what may be buried beneath the newly accessible territories.

Aldwin initially sends the player to investigate reports of ancient magical activity.

His relationship with the player changes depending on the player's actions throughout Act I.

### Personality

Calm, analytical, diplomatic, and politically cautious.

He rarely raises his voice.

When challenged, he tends to respond with questions rather than threats.

### Character Conflict

Aldwin genuinely believes the Concord can prevent another collapse.

His weakness is that he sometimes assumes the institutions of the old world can solve problems created by the new one.

---

# Warmother Ushka Dren

**Role:** Ruler / Military Leader
**Faction:** Duskward Pact
**Kingdom:** Ashenclaw Warbands
**Location:** Ashka Vor
**Questline:** Main Story — Act II

Warmother Ushka Dren leads the Ashenclaw Warcouncil.

She is one of the most influential political figures in Vethmoor.

Ushka strongly supports Duskward independence.

She believes the people of Elysium survived the Sundering by adapting rather than rebuilding the world that existed before it.

She distrusts Concord expeditions entering Vethmoor.

Despite this, she does not automatically oppose the player.

If the player demonstrates respect for Ashenclaw territory, Ushka becomes a valuable ally.

### Personality

Direct, disciplined, suspicious, and protective.

She has little patience for political speeches.

She judges people primarily by what they do.

### Character Conflict

Ushka wants to protect the Ashenclaw from outside control.

But her hostility toward the Concord sometimes makes cooperation more difficult than it needs to be.

---

# Scholar Peren Vale

**Role:** Scholar / Researcher
**Faction:** Ashen Circle
**Location:** Varies
**Questline:** Main Story — Acts II–III

Peren Vale is an Ashen Circle researcher specializing in Sundering-era magical phenomena.

He becomes the player's primary scholarly contact during the investigation into the ancient ruins.

Unlike many members of the Circle, Peren is willing to work directly with adventurers.

He believes that historical evidence should be available to everyone.

His research eventually leads him to Aethercrest.

### Personality

Curious, obsessive, intelligent, and occasionally reckless.

Peren has a habit of continuing an investigation long after everyone else has decided it is too dangerous.

### Character Conflict

Peren wants the truth about the Sundering.

He gradually realizes that discovering the truth and surviving it may be two different problems.

# Regional NPCs

Regional characters should make individual areas feel culturally distinct.

A region should contain a mixture of:

* Local leaders
* Merchants
* Farmers
* Soldiers
* Scholars
* Religious figures
* Craftspeople
* Criminals
* Travelers
* Adventurers
* Children
* Elderly residents
* Faction representatives

Not every NPC should care about the major political conflict.

---

# Aurelia NPCs

Aurelia contains many of the oldest surviving human settlements.

NPCs here should reflect the region's position as one of the primary centers of Dawnbound reconstruction.

Common character themes include:

* Rebuilding
* Trade
* Historical nostalgia
* Political disagreement
* Exploration
* Human kingdoms
* High elven scholarship
* Religious tradition

---

# Concord Dominion NPCs

Concord Dominion characters generally represent the political structure of Solmere and surrounding territories.

Possible roles include:

* Council representatives
* Soldiers
* Magistrates
* Merchants
* Scholars
* Diplomats
* Engineers
* Adventurers

Not all Dominion NPCs should support the Concord's policies.

Political disagreement should exist within the Dominion itself.

---

# Sylvaria NPCs

High elven NPCs should reflect the Enclave's long history and access to Age of Concord knowledge.

Common themes include:

* Magic
* Scholarship
* Preservation
* Isolation
* Elven identity
* Ancient history
* The responsibility of knowledge

Some Sylvarian characters should support the Dawnbound philosophy.

Others should question whether the Age of Concord deserves to be restored.

---

# Silverwatch NPCs

Silverwatch characters should represent one of Aurelia's major regional powers.

Possible roles include:

* Watch commanders
* Rangers
* Magistrates
* Scouts
* Merchants
* Landowners
* Soldiers
* Local nobles
* Frontier settlers

Silverwatch NPCs should generally value stability and regional security.

They may support the Dawnbound Concord while still disagreeing with Solmere.

---

# Vethmoor NPCs

Vethmoor characters should reflect the continent's harsher environment and stronger clan-based cultures.

Common themes include:

* Survival
* Clan loyalty
* Independence
* Ancestral traditions
* Ancient ruins
* Mining
* War
* Honor
* Territorial disputes

---

# Ironpeak NPCs

Dwarven NPCs should reflect the political structure of the Ironpeak Holds.

Common roles include:

* Clan leaders
* Forgemasters
* Miners
* Engineers
* Smiths
* Historians
* Tunnel guards
* Merchants

Dwarven NPCs should not all behave identically.

Some should be traditionalists.

Others should favor technological experimentation.

Some should strongly support the Dawnbound Concord.

Others should prefer complete dwarven independence.

---

# Ashenclaw NPCs

Orc NPCs should reflect the clan structure of the Ashenclaw Warbands.

Common roles include:

* Clan chiefs
* War leaders
* Hunters
* Warriors
* Shamans
* Scouts
* Blacksmiths
* Diplomats

Ashenclaw characters should value clan identity and independence.

The player should encounter significant differences between individual clans.

There should not be a single "orc personality."

---

# Beastkin NPCs

Beastkin cultures should become increasingly important as later continents are introduced.

Beastkin NPCs should represent different clans, environments, and traditions.

They should not be treated as a single culture.

Different Beastkin groups may have completely different:

* Religions
* Languages
* Political structures
* Clothing
* Architecture
* Attitudes toward other races

---

# Revenant NPCs

Revenant characters should provide some of the most direct perspectives on the consequences of the Sundering.

They may have:

* Memories of the Age of Ash
* Family histories connected to the Sundering
* Experiences with discrimination
* Relationships with Voranthe
* Knowledge of ancient locations
* Different opinions about their own condition

Revenant NPCs should not all be mysterious or grim.

Some should simply be ordinary people living ordinary lives.

---

# Neutral NPCs

Neutral organizations provide characters who can interact with both major factions.

## Wayfarer's Guild

Possible recurring characters:

* Guildmasters
* Couriers
* Merchants
* Caravan leaders
* Auctioneers
* Route planners

The Guild should generally avoid political allegiance.

Its primary concern is keeping trade moving.

---

## Cartographer's League

Possible recurring characters:

* Cartographers
* Archaeologists
* Historians
* Explorers
* Surveyors
* Researchers

The League provides many exploration-focused stories.

---

## Ashen Circle

Possible recurring characters:

* Scholars
* Researchers
* Archivists
* Magical investigators
* Historians

The Circle should remain secretive.

Not every member should agree about how much information should be revealed.

# Companion Design

Companions should have independent character arcs.

A companion should have:

```text
Introduction
      ↓
Relationship Development
      ↓
Personal Conflict
      ↓
Character Decision
      ↓
Resolution
```

A companion should not remain permanently static.

Their opinions can change based on the player's actions.

They may disagree with the player.

They may leave temporarily.

They may return later.

They may become rivals.

They may die if the story specifically calls for it.

Deaths should be meaningful rather than used for shock value.

---

# NPC Relationships

Important NPCs should have relationships with other NPCs.

Examples:

```text
Aldwin Rae
   │
   ├── Political ally → Silverwatch
   ├── Diplomatic ally → Ironpeak Holds
   └── Political rival → Ushka Dren
```

Or:

```text
Peren Vale
   │
   ├── Research partner → Ashen Circle
   ├── Rival → Concord historian
   └── Student → Young Arcanist
```

NPC relationships should help make the world feel interconnected.

---

# NPC Dialogue

Dialogue should communicate character rather than simply provide information.

NPCs should have:

* Distinct vocabulary
* Different speaking styles
* Personal opinions
* Regional expressions
* Knowledge limitations
* Emotional reactions

NPCs should not explain information they have no reason to know.

A farmer should not casually explain the entire history of the Sundering.

A scholar might.

An orc elder might know an oral tradition that contradicts the scholar.

Both can be useful sources.

---

# NPC Knowledge

NPCs should not automatically know the truth.

Information should depend on:

* Profession
* Culture
* Age
* Location
* Faction
* Education
* Personal experience

This allows different NPCs to provide different perspectives on the same event.

---

# Recurring NPCs

Important NPCs should be allowed to appear outside their original questline.

A character introduced in Aurelia may later appear in Vethmoor.

A merchant might move between cities.

A scholar may appear at multiple ruins.

A companion may return during a later expansion.

Recurring characters should maintain continuity.

They should remember major events involving the player where technically practical.

---

# NPC Naming

Names should reflect the culture and region of the character.

Naming conventions should be established for each major race and culture.

Avoid giving every character a generic fantasy name.

Names should also avoid being excessively complicated simply to sound fantasy-like.

A good name should be memorable and readable.

---

# NPC Consistency

Every major named NPC should have a record containing:

```text
Name:
Race:
Age:
Role:
Faction:
Kingdom:
Home:
First Appearance:
Questline:
Personality:
Goals:
Relationships:
Known History:
Current Status:
```

The record should be updated whenever the character's story changes.

---

# NPC Writing Standard

Every important NPC should answer three questions:

### Who are they?

Their identity, role, culture, and background.

### What do they want?

Their immediate and long-term goals.

### Why should the player care?

Their connection to the world, the player, another character, or a meaningful conflict.

If an NPC cannot answer these questions, they should probably remain a generic NPC rather than receiving a unique name and story.

---

# Core Principle

NPCs should make Elysium feel populated by people rather than quest dispensers.

The player is not the only person living through the Age of Reclamation.

Every city has people with their own problems.

Every faction has internal disagreements.

Every kingdom has competing interests.

Every culture has different interpretations of history.

The main story is what the player experiences.

NPC stories are what make the world exist when the player is not looking.
