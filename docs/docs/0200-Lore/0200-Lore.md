# 0200 — Lore

## Overview

Elysium is a world shaped by creation, cooperation, destruction, and the long struggle to rebuild what was lost.

Its history is divided into Ages, each defined by a fundamental change in the relationship between gods, mortals, and the world itself.

The oldest stories speak of the **Age of Dawn**, when the seven gods of the Elysian Circle first shaped the world and gave its peoples a place within it.

The Age of Dawn eventually gave way to the **Age of Concord**, a period remembered as the greatest era of cooperation in Elysian history. Human kingdoms, high elven courts, dwarven holds, and countless other cultures flourished together. Magic was studied openly, cities stretched across entire regions, and the boundaries between the mortal world and the divine seemed almost nonexistent.

But the Concord was not eternal.

An eighth god, **Kaelgorath**, came to believe that the mortal world had become something it was never meant to be. He saw mortals choosing their own paths instead of following the designs of their creators. To him, free will was not a gift. It was a flaw.

His rebellion became the **Sundering**.

The conflict destroyed the old world, scattered the continents, shattered civilizations, and silenced the gods.

What followed was the **Age of Ash**, centuries of survival in a world that no longer resembled the one known by the generations before it.

Now, eight centuries after the Sundering, Elysium has entered the **Age of Reclamation**.

The continents are slowly reconnecting.

Old roads are becoming usable again.

Ancient ruins are being uncovered.

Forgotten magic is returning.

New kingdoms are rising.

And the people of Elysium are being forced to decide what kind of world they actually want to build.

Some believe the old world should be restored.

Others believe it deserved to fall.

Neither side knows the full truth.

---

# The Beginning

Before recorded history, Elysium was not a world of kingdoms, cities, or mortal nations.

It was raw and unfinished.

The seven gods of the **Elysian Circle** shaped it according to their domains.

Solthar brought light.

Nyxara brought night.

Terravox raised mountains and carved the foundations of the earth.

Maelithir filled the world with oceans and storms.

Ignareth brought fire and the power to transform what already existed.

Voranthe established the boundaries between life, death, and fate.

Aeloria filled the world with life.

Together, they created a world that could sustain mortal life.

They did not create a perfect world.

They created a world capable of change.

The gods then shaped the first mortal peoples and gave them the freedom to develop their own cultures.

The gods did not rule every kingdom.

They did not dictate every decision.

Instead, they watched.

Mortals built.

Mortals fought.

Mortals traded.

Mortals explored.

Mortals created things their gods had never imagined.

This freedom would eventually become the greatest source of conflict in Elysium.

---

# The Age of Dawn

The first major era of mortal civilization became known as the **Age of Dawn**.

Human settlements spread across Aurelia.

The first high elven kingdoms emerged.

Dwarven ancestors established settlements beneath the mountains.

Early orc clans developed in the northern regions.

The ancestors of the Beastkin formed countless independent clans.

The first Revenants would not appear until much later.

During this era, the gods were still directly involved with the mortal world.

Shrines were places of communication rather than simply places of worship.

Priests could receive visions.

Relics could carry genuine divine power.

Some mortals were chosen directly by the gods and became legendary champions.

Magic was also relatively unstable.

The world itself was still young, and the boundaries between the physical world, the divine realms, and the forces of magic had not yet fully settled.

Many ancient ruins found throughout Elysium date from this period.

Most of their original purpose has been forgotten.

---

# The Age of Concord

Eventually, the scattered civilizations of Elysium began to interact on a larger scale.

Trade routes connected distant settlements.

Knowledge passed between cultures.

The great cities were established.

The races that had once lived independently began to cooperate.

This period became known as the **Age of Concord**.

It is remembered as Elysium's golden age.

The great capitals were constructed.

Solmere became one of the largest cities in the known world.

The Ironpeak Holds expanded beneath the mountains.

High elven academies became centers of magical research.

Human kingdoms developed complex systems of law and government.

Dwarven smiths created weapons and machines that were considered impossible by earlier generations.

Explorers crossed oceans that had previously been thought impassable.

Magic became a formal discipline rather than something understood only by isolated scholars.

The six great class traditions eventually emerged from these cultures.

Vanguards became protectors and soldiers.

Arcanists studied the fundamental forces of magic.

Shades mastered secrecy, espionage, and the manipulation of shadow.

Wardens protected the natural world.

Oathkeepers served as guardians bound by sacred promises.

Wayfarers became explorers, scouts, hunters, and guides through the world's dangerous frontier.

The classes were never simply occupations.

They were traditions.

Each carried its own philosophies, techniques, and relationships with the gods.

---

# The Problem of the Concord

The Age of Concord is remembered as a golden age.

It was not perfect.

The old world was still divided by borders, politics, religion, and culture.

Not every race had equal influence.

Not every kingdom trusted its neighbors.

There were wars.

There were rebellions.

There were conflicts over magic, territory, resources, and religion.

But compared with what came afterward, the Concord appeared almost perfect.

This created a dangerous idea among later generations.

The belief that the old world could simply be restored.

That idea would eventually become one of the central conflicts of modern Elysium.

---

# Kaelgorath

Among the Elysian Circle was an eighth god.

His name was **Kaelgorath**.

He was not originally considered a destroyer.

He was a creator.

He helped shape the world alongside the other gods.

But Kaelgorath became increasingly dissatisfied with the relationship between gods and mortals.

The other gods believed mortals should be free.

Kaelgorath disagreed.

He believed the gods had created Elysium, and therefore had the right to determine what happened within it.

To Kaelgorath, mortal independence represented a rejection of their creators.

He watched kingdoms rise and fall.

He watched mortals reject religious traditions.

He watched scholars challenge divine teachings.

He watched mortals create magic the gods had never given them.

And eventually, he concluded that Elysium had become corrupted by its own freedom.

He wanted to correct it.

At first, he tried to convince the other gods.

They refused.

The Circle turned against him.

Kaelgorath interpreted their refusal as betrayal.

The conflict between the gods became inevitable.

---

# The Sundering

The war between Kaelgorath and the Elysian Circle began in the final years of the Age of Concord.

Mortals initially knew almost nothing about it.

Strange storms appeared.

Magic behaved unpredictably.

Entire regions experienced earthquakes without warning.

The gods became increasingly silent.

Then the conflict reached the mortal world.

Kaelgorath attempted to tear apart the foundations of Elysium itself.

The Circle intervened.

The resulting battle became known as the **Sundering**.

The consequences were catastrophic.

Continents broke apart.

Mountain ranges shifted.

Seas flooded ancient kingdoms.

Cities disappeared beneath the earth.

Magic erupted across the world.

Entire civilizations were erased within days.

The boundaries between life and death were damaged.

Some mortals were permanently changed by the energy released during the conflict.

These people would eventually become known as the **Revenants**.

The gods fought Kaelgorath directly.

But they could not defeat him without destroying Elysium in the process.

Instead, Kaelgorath was scattered.

His body, power, and consciousness were torn apart.

He became the **Sundered One**.

The Circle survived.

But the gods could no longer remain directly connected to the mortal world.

One by one, their voices disappeared.

The age of divine intervention ended.

---

# The Age of Ash

The centuries following the Sundering became known as the **Age of Ash**.

The world was broken.

The old roads were gone.

The great capitals were destroyed.

Entire peoples were separated from one another.

Survivors gathered wherever they could.

Some lived inside the ruins of old cities.

Others established settlements around surviving fortifications.

Many abandoned the old religions entirely.

For generations, survival was more important than history.

Knowledge was lost.

Languages changed.

Old technologies disappeared.

Magic became dangerous.

The descendants of the old civilizations no longer knew exactly what their ancestors had built.

The world became smaller.

People stopped crossing oceans.

Travel between continents became almost impossible.

The old maps became useless.

What had once been a connected civilization became a collection of isolated societies.

---

# The Silence of the Gods

The most painful consequence of the Sundering was not the destruction of the cities.

It was the silence.

The gods never spoke directly to mortals again.

Shrines remained.

Temples remained.

Relics remained.

Stories remained.

But there were no more divine voices.

Priests could pray for years without receiving an answer.

Some believed the gods had abandoned them.

Others believed the gods were dead.

Others believed they were trapped somewhere beyond the mortal world.

Some began to worship the memory of the gods rather than the gods themselves.

Religion changed from direct communication into tradition.

This created countless interpretations of the Circle.

Two people could worship Solthar and have completely different beliefs about what Solthar actually wanted.

This remains true in the present day.

---

# The Revenants

The Sundering left more than ruins behind.

Some mortals survived exposure to the energy released during the destruction.

Their bodies changed.

Their skin became pale.

Their bodies became unnaturally cold.

Some stopped aging normally.

Others developed strange connections to death, memory, or fate.

These people became known as the **Revenants**.

They were not undead.

They were still mortal.

But they were no longer entirely unchanged.

Many societies feared them.

Some kingdoms expelled them.

Others attempted to study them.

A few communities accepted them.

Over generations, Revenants developed their own cultures and traditions.

Their existence remains one of the clearest reminders that the Sundering was not simply a historical event.

Its effects are still alive.

---

# The Surviving Civilizations

Not every civilization disappeared.

Some survived in fragmented form.

Human kingdoms endured in Aurelia.

Dwarven settlements survived within the Ironpeak Holds.

Orc clans established themselves across the Ashenclaw Tundra.

High elven communities preserved fragments of their ancient knowledge.

Beastkin clans survived across remote regions.

Revenant communities developed around areas heavily affected by the Sundering.

But these societies were no longer connected.

Each developed independently.

This created the cultures that exist in modern Elysium.

Many of them remember the Age of Concord differently.

Some remember it as a golden age.

Some remember it as an age of inequality.

Some barely remember it at all.

---

# The Return

Eventually, the world began to change again.

The seas became calmer.

Old magical barriers weakened.

Previously isolated regions began appearing on distant horizons.

Ruins became accessible.

Ancient roads resurfaced.

Ships began reaching places that had been unreachable for centuries.

The continents were slowly reconnecting.

The process was not natural.

Something had changed.

Nobody knew exactly what.

But the people of Elysium began returning to the places their ancestors had abandoned.

This marked the beginning of the **Age of Reclamation**.

---

# The Age of Reclamation

The present era is defined by reconstruction.

Old cities are being rebuilt.

New settlements are appearing around ancient ruins.

Explorers are mapping previously inaccessible territories.

Merchants are reopening trade routes.

Scholars are recovering lost knowledge.

Adventurers are entering ruins that have remained untouched since the Sundering.

But rebuilding the old world has created a fundamental political disagreement.

What should Elysium become?

---

# The Dawnbound Concord

The **Dawnbound Concord** believes the old civilization should be restored.

Its members see the Age of Concord as proof that different peoples can build a prosperous world together.

They want to restore the old capitals.

They want to reopen ancient trade routes.

They want to recover lost knowledge.

They want to rebuild the institutions that existed before the Sundering.

To the Concord, the old world was not the problem.

The Sundering was.

Their belief can be summarized simply:

**"Restore what was lost."**

The Concord is particularly influential among humans, high elves, and dwarves.

Its political center is **Solmere**.

But the Concord is not a single kingdom.

It is a coalition.

Regional powers such as **Silverwatch** may support the Concord while maintaining their own governments, territories, military forces, and interests.

This sometimes creates tension between the ideals of the Concord and the realities of individual regions.

---

# The Duskward Pact

The **Duskward Pact** sees the past differently.

Its members believe the old world failed.

The Age of Concord may have been prosperous, but it eventually collapsed.

Restoring the same systems that existed before the Sundering, they argue, would simply recreate the conditions that allowed history to repeat itself.

The Pact wants to build something new.

Its members value independence, strength, self-reliance, and alliances between peoples that were once excluded from the old order.

Its influence is particularly strong among orcs, Beastkin, and Revenants.

Its political center is **Ashka Vor**.

The Pact does not necessarily hate the old world.

It simply refuses to worship it.

Its belief can be summarized as:

**"Build what comes next."**

---

# A Divided World

The conflict between the Concord and Pact is not simply a war over territory.

It is a disagreement about history.

Both sides want Elysium to survive.

Both sides believe they are preventing another Sundering.

Both sides believe the other side is making the same mistakes that caused the old world to collapse.

The Concord sees the Pact as reckless.

The Pact sees the Concord as trapped in the past.

Neither side has the complete truth.

And neither knows what the gods would actually want.

---

# The Regional Powers

The two major factions do not control every part of Elysium.

Across the continents are dozens of kingdoms, clans, city-states, military orders, tribes, and independent territories.

Some support the Concord.

Some support the Pact.

Some remain neutral.

Some simply want to survive.

**Silverwatch**, for example, is a powerful regional faction aligned with the Dawnbound Concord.

Its loyalty to the Concord does not erase its own identity.

The same principle applies throughout Elysium.

A person may belong to the Concord while identifying more strongly with their homeland.

A kingdom may support the Pact while disagreeing with its leadership.

A city may officially remain neutral while secretly supplying one side.

This creates a world where political allegiance is rarely simple.

---

# The Neutral Organizations

Not everyone wants to participate in the conflict.

Organizations such as the **Wayfarer's Guild** operate across faction borders.

Merchants need trade.

Travelers need roads.

Explorers need maps.

Scholars need access to ruins.

Because of this, neutral organizations often become some of the few places where members of opposing factions can interact peacefully.

The **Cartographer's League** documents the changing world.

The **Wayfarer's Guild** keeps trade moving.

The **Ashen Circle** studies the remaining influence of Kaelgorath.

These organizations often know things that the major factions do not.

Some may eventually become involved in conflicts far larger than their original purpose.

---

# The Return of Magic

Magic never disappeared after the Sundering.

It changed.

Some forms became unstable.

Some were forgotten.

Some became stronger.

Ancient magical sites began activating again during the Age of Reclamation.

Artifacts thought to be dead began functioning.

Old spells resurfaced.

Creatures that had not been seen for centuries began appearing.

The reason for this remains unclear.

Some scholars believe the continents reconnecting is restoring the world's magical structure.

Others believe the gods are returning.

Others believe Kaelgorath is waking.

There may be truth in all three theories.

---

# The Corruption

Across Elysium, certain regions show signs of a darker force.

Plants grow unnaturally.

Animals mutate.

Magic becomes unstable.

Dead creatures sometimes refuse to remain dead.

People report hearing voices from places where nobody is present.

These regions are often connected to fragments of Kaelgorath's remaining influence.

The **Ashen Circle** has spent years studying these phenomena.

Their findings suggest something deeply troubling.

Kaelgorath may have been scattered.

But he was not destroyed.

His influence still exists.

And the more the world reconnects, the stronger those fragments appear to become.

---

# The Lost Truth

The official history of the Sundering is incomplete.

Most surviving records were written generations after the event.

Many were written by people who never witnessed it.

Some records contradict one another.

Some ancient artifacts suggest the gods were not united during the final battle.

Others imply Kaelgorath may not have acted entirely alone.

There are ruins containing symbols that do not belong to any known culture.

There are ancient weapons whose creators have been forgotten.

There are places where time, magic, and memory appear to behave differently.

The truth of the Sundering has never been fully recovered.

The people of Elysium have inherited a history full of gaps.

And someone, or something, may have wanted those gaps to remain.

---

# The Role of the Player

The player enters Elysium during the early Age of Reclamation.

They are not the chosen one.

They are not automatically a descendant of an ancient hero.

They are one person entering a world that is already moving.

Their actions can change local conflicts.

They can earn the trust of regional factions.

They can explore forgotten ruins.

They can uncover lost histories.

They can fight alongside the Dawnbound Concord or Duskward Pact.

They can work with neutral organizations.

They can become enemies of entire kingdoms.

They can discover truths that major factions would rather remain hidden.

The player does not begin by knowing the truth about Elysium.

They discover it through the world.

---

# The Six Traditions

The major classes of Elysium are not simply combat roles.

They are traditions that developed from the civilizations of the old world and survived the Sundering.

### Vanguard

Vanguards are protectors, soldiers, and frontline warriors.

Their traditions emphasize physical strength, endurance, discipline, and protecting others.

They are commonly associated with Solthar, Terravox, and Ignareth.

### Arcanist

Arcanists study the forces that govern magic.

They seek to understand what happened during the Sundering and how magic itself functions.

Their traditions are associated with Maelithir, Ignareth, and Voranthe.

### Shade

Shades operate where others cannot see.

They specialize in stealth, deception, information, and the manipulation of shadow.

Their traditions are associated with Nyxara and Voranthe.

### Warden

Wardens protect the natural world.

They study beasts, plants, ancient forests, and the forces that keep nature in balance.

Their traditions are strongly associated with Aeloria.

### Oathkeeper

Oathkeepers bind themselves to a purpose.

Their strength comes from discipline, conviction, and sacred promises.

They are traditionally associated with Solthar and Terravox.

### Wayfarer

Wayfarers travel where roads end.

They are scouts, explorers, hunters, trackers, and guides.

Their traditions are associated with Nyxara, Maelithir, and Aeloria.

The six traditions are not restricted to particular races or factions.

A person's class is a personal path.

Their race and political allegiance may influence their relationship with that tradition, but neither determines it.

---

# The Continents

Elysium's continents were once connected.

The Sundering separated them.

The Age of Reclamation is slowly reversing that separation.

### Aurelia

The heartland of human civilization and one of the most important centers of the Dawnbound Concord.

Aurelia contains many of the surviving traces of the old world.

It is also where many of the earliest reclamation efforts began.

### Vethmoor

A harsh continent dominated by mountains, tundra, and ancient settlements.

The Ironpeak Holds and Ashenclaw Tundra are among its most important regions.

Dwarves and orcs have shaped much of its modern political identity.

### Sylvaneth

A future continent dominated by ancient forests and the descendants of old elven civilizations.

Its history is closely connected to nature, ancient magic, and the disappearance of the old wood elf cultures.

### Kharzul Wastes

A future continent dominated by the Beastkin.

The Wastes contain harsh environments, ancient ruins, and cultures that developed largely outside the influence of the old Concord.

### Nightreach

A future continent heavily affected by corruption.

Its peoples and ecosystems have been changed by forces connected to the deeper mysteries of Kaelgorath.

The continent represents one of the clearest signs that the Sundering is not simply a historical event.

Its consequences are still unfolding.

---

# The Central Question

Everything in Elysium eventually leads back to one question:

**What should the world become?**

The Dawnbound Concord wants to restore.

The Duskward Pact wants to rebuild differently.

Regional powers want independence.

Neutral organizations want stability.

Scholars want answers.

The gods remain silent.

Kaelgorath remains scattered.

And the continents are reconnecting.

The people of Elysium have spent eight centuries surviving the consequences of the Sundering.

Now they have to decide what comes after survival.

The old world is returning.

But it may not be the world anyone remembers.

---

# The Future of Elysium

The story of Elysium is not about returning to the past.

It is about deciding what deserves to survive from it.

Ancient kingdoms may return.

New kingdoms may rise.

Old alliances may collapse.

New factions may emerge.

The gods may remain silent.

Or they may finally speak again.

The fragments of Kaelgorath may be destroyed.

Or they may become something far worse.

The Age of Reclamation has only begun.

Elysium is no longer waiting for history to repeat itself.

It is creating a new history.

And for the first time since the Sundering, the future belongs entirely to mortals.

---

## Themes

Elysium's narrative is built around several central themes.

### Reconstruction vs. Nostalgia

The world must be rebuilt, but rebuilding the past does not necessarily mean repeating it.

### Faith Without Gods

The Elysian Circle has been silent since the Sundering.

Mortals must decide what their gods mean without being able to ask them.

### Fragmentation and Reunion

The physical world mirrors the people living within it.

Broken continents reconnect.

Broken nations reunite.

Broken families find one another.

Old enemies become allies.

New conflicts emerge.

### Freedom vs. Control

Kaelgorath believed mortals could not be trusted with freedom.

The modern world must prove whether he was wrong.

### Memory vs. Truth

History has been rewritten, forgotten, destroyed, and misunderstood.

The stories people inherit are not always the truth.

### The Past vs. The Future

The Dawnbound Concord and Duskward Pact represent two different answers to the same problem.

One asks:

**"How do we restore what we lost?"**

The other asks:

**"Why should we rebuild the world that failed?"**

Neither answer is guaranteed to be correct.
