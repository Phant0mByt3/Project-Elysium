# 1. Overview

Mythology in Elysium exists between history, religion, folklore, and speculation.

Some myths describe events that genuinely happened.

Others are distorted versions of historical events.

Some are deliberate political propaganda.

Others may be completely fictional.

The difficulty is that nobody can reliably tell which is which.

The Sundering destroyed enormous amounts of written history.

The centuries that followed introduced oral traditions, regional interpretations, religious doctrines, political propaganda, and simple human error.

As a result, Elysium's oldest stories rarely exist in a single version.

A High Elven scholar may describe an event one way.

A Dwarven historian may describe the same event differently.

An Orcish clan may have preserved an entirely different version through generations of oral tradition.

All three may contain pieces of the truth.

---

# 2. Myth and History

Elysian scholars generally divide stories into three broad categories.

### Recorded History

Events supported by surviving physical evidence, documents, or multiple independent sources.

### Historical Tradition

Stories passed through generations that probably contain historical events but have been altered over time.

### Myth

Stories whose historical accuracy cannot currently be established.

These categories are not permanent.

A myth can become history when new evidence is discovered.

Likewise, an accepted historical account can become questionable when new evidence contradicts it.

This is particularly important when researching the Age of Dawn and the Age of Concord.

---

# 3. The Shaping of Elysium

The oldest widespread myth claims that the Elysian Circle created the world from an endless void.

According to the story, each god contributed something essential.

Solthar brought light.

Nyxara brought darkness and the mysteries hidden within it.

Terravox shaped the foundations of the world.

Maelithir filled the world with seas and storms.

Ignareth gave mortals fire and the ability to transform materials.

Voranthe established death and the passage of souls.

Aeloria gave the world life and growth.

Together, they shaped Elysium.

The gods then created the mortal races.

The story differs dramatically between cultures.

Humans often describe the gods as creators.

Dwarves describe them more often as builders.

High Elven traditions describe them as cosmic forces.

Orcish traditions describe the creation of the world as a great forging.

Revenant traditions often question why death was created before mortals understood what it meant.

No surviving record can establish which version, if any, is literally correct.

---

# 4. The First Mortal

Many cultures possess a story about the first mortal.

The identity of this figure changes depending on the storyteller.

Human tradition calls them **Elyan**.

Some High Elven texts call them **Vael**.

Several Dwarven traditions call them **First-Forged**.

Some Orcish clans claim the first mortal was simply the first warrior to stand against the gods.

The stories share one important detail.

The first mortal was given a choice.

The gods could provide guidance, protection, and purpose.

The mortal instead chose freedom.

This moment is commonly considered the origin of mortal free will.

It is also frequently cited as the beginning of Kaelgorath's resentment.

---

# 5. The Eightfold Myth

Some ancient stories claim that the Elysian Circle originally consisted of eight gods.

The seventh known gods reject this interpretation.

According to the myth, Kaelgorath was once an equal member of the Circle.

He was associated with concepts that have since been deliberately erased from most surviving religious texts.

Possible interpretations include:

* Knowledge
* Ambition
* Change
* Creation
* Will
* Void
* Dominion

No surviving source provides a definitive answer.

The absence of a confirmed domain has led to centuries of speculation.

The Ashen Circle considers the missing domain one of the most important unanswered questions in Elysian history.

---

# 6. The First Sundering Prophecy

Several fragments of pre-Sundering writing contain references to an event sometimes called the **First Sundering**.

One translation reads:

> "When the eighth will rises against the seven, the world shall remember that it was never whole."

The authenticity of these fragments is heavily disputed.

Some scholars believe they describe Kaelgorath's eventual rebellion.

Others believe they were written after the Sundering and falsely presented as ancient prophecy.

The Ashen Circle considers both possibilities plausible.

If genuine, the fragments suggest that at least some ancient scholars predicted the Sundering.

---

# 7. The Fall of Aethercrest

The destruction of Aethercrest has become one of Elysium's most famous myths.

The commonly accepted story states that Kaelgorath struck the united capital directly, causing the city to collapse into the earth.

But the exact details differ.

Human accounts describe a column of darkness descending upon the city.

High Elven records describe the sky itself breaking apart.

Dwarven records describe the ground moving like molten metal.

Orcish oral tradition describes a battle between gods that lasted seven days.

Revenant traditions contain an especially disturbing version.

They claim that the dead of Aethercrest continued walking for three days after the city had already fallen.

Whether this is historical fact remains unknown.

---

# 8. The Seven Last Words

One widespread legend claims that immediately before the gods disappeared, each member of the Elysian Circle spoke one final sentence.

No two surviving traditions agree on what those words were.

One version claims:

**Solthar:** "Rise again."

**Nyxara:** "Remember what you cannot see."

**Terravox:** "Endure."

**Maelithir:** "Nothing remains still."

**Ignareth:** "Become stronger."

**Voranthe:** "All roads end."

**Aeloria:** "Life continues."

Other traditions provide completely different words.

Some scholars believe the story is entirely symbolic.

Others believe the original words may have been deliberately hidden.

---

# 9. The Silence

One of the most enduring myths concerns what happened to the gods after the Sundering.

There are four major interpretations.

### The Sleeping Gods

The gods survived but were wounded and entered a deep sleep.

### The Abandoned World

The gods survived but deliberately left Elysium.

### The Broken Circle

The gods survived as fragmented remnants of their former selves.

### The Mortal Age

The gods died, and Elysium now belongs entirely to mortals.

None has been proven.

Religious traditions frequently favor one interpretation.

Scholars generally consider all four possible.

---

# 10. The Drifting Continents

One of the most common myths of the Age of Reclamation describes the continents as living fragments of a broken world.

According to the legend, the continents are slowly moving back toward one another because Elysium itself remembers being whole.

Cartographers reject the literal interpretation.

They have measured the movement and generally consider it a physical consequence of the magical forces left behind by the Sundering.

Poets, priests, and storytellers continue to use the old metaphor.

The phrase:

**"The world is finding its way home."**

has become common throughout Elysium.

---

# 11. The Lost Capital

Some legends claim that Aethercrest was not destroyed.

Instead, the city supposedly exists beneath the world, preserved in a hidden realm.

According to different versions, it is:

* Beneath the Shattered Cairns.
* Beneath the ocean.
* Frozen in time.
* Trapped between worlds.
* Protected by the gods.
* Slowly rebuilding itself.

The discovery of the Sunken Concord has disproven some versions while making others considerably more interesting.

The ruins are real.

The complete city has never been found.

---

# 12. The Seven Relics

A popular legend claims that each god left behind a relic containing a fragment of their power.

The supposed relics include:

* The Dawnshard
* The Moonveil
* The Heartstone
* The Storm Crown
* The First Flame
* The Final Hourglass
* The Seed of Eternity

No complete collection has ever been discovered.

Some of these objects have almost certainly existed.

Others may have been invented centuries later.

Collectors, adventurers, scholars, and treasure hunters continue to search for them.

---

# 13. The Eighth Relic

A darker variation of the Seven Relics legend claims that Kaelgorath also left behind a relic.

It is commonly called:

**The Eighth Fragment.**

Unlike the other relics, it supposedly does not grant power.

It grants knowledge.

The legend claims that anyone who understands it will learn why Kaelgorath rebelled.

Most scholars consider the story dangerous nonsense.

The Ashen Circle disagrees.

---

# 14. The Weeping Hollow

The Greywater Fens contain one of the region's most persistent legends.

According to local stories, two warriors fought each other in the Weeping Hollow during the final days of the Age of Concord.

Neither could defeat the other.

When the Sundering occurred, their duel supposedly continued.

Travelers occasionally report seeing two spectral figures fighting in the mist.

Neither attacks travelers.

Neither speaks.

They simply fight.

Some believe they are the ghosts of ancient soldiers.

Others believe they represent two opposing philosophies trapped in an eternal struggle.

No confirmed explanation exists.

---

# 15. The Sundered Spire

The Sundered Spire is said to have been a magical tower belonging to an ancient High Elven order.

According to legend, the tower was struck by the Sundering while its mages were performing a powerful ritual.

The entire structure froze in the moment of collapse.

Broken stones remain suspended in the air.

Ancient magical energy still flows through the ruins.

Some believe the mages were attempting to stop the Sundering.

Others believe they caused part of it.

The truth remains unknown.

---

# 16. The Cairn of Names

The Cairn of Names in the Shattered Cairns region is associated with one of the oldest traditions of the Age of Ash.

Survivors who could not recover the bodies of their dead carved their names into stones.

As generations passed, the cairn grew.

Eventually, people began adding the names of those lost in wars, disasters, expeditions, and disappearances.

A common tradition is to add a stone whenever someone dies without a known grave.

The practice eventually spread across Elysium.

The phrase:

**"Give them a stone."**

became synonymous with remembering the dead.

---

# 17. The Nameless King

A popular story among travelers tells of a king who ruled shortly before the Sundering.

His name has been lost.

His kingdom has been lost.

His tomb has never been found.

According to the legend, he knew the Sundering was coming but refused to warn his people because he believed civilization would collapse regardless.

Another version claims he tried to save everyone and failed.

A third claims he survived the Sundering and still rules somewhere beyond the known continents.

Historians have never confirmed that the Nameless King even existed.

---

# 18. The Ashenclaw Firstfire

Orcish clans tell a story about the first clan-fire.

The legend claims that after the Sundering, the surviving orcs wandered through a frozen wilderness with no shelter and no food.

An unknown warrior found a single surviving ember.

They carried it for seven days.

Every clan supposedly descended from the people who gathered around that fire.

Different clans argue about who the warrior was.

Some claim it was their ancestor.

Others say the story is symbolic.

The fire itself remains a central symbol of Orcish unity.

---

# 19. The First Forge

Dwarven tradition claims that the first forge after the Sundering was built by a group of survivors trapped beneath the mountains.

They possessed almost no food and almost no weapons.

Instead of attempting to escape, they began rebuilding.

The first object they forged was supposedly a hammer.

The hammer was never intended as a weapon.

It was used to rebuild the gates of their settlement.

The story became the foundation of the Stone Covenant's belief that rebuilding is itself an act of survival.

---

# 20. The Revenant's Dream

A common Revenant legend claims that every Revenant eventually dreams of the same place.

A black ocean.

A ruined city.

Seven distant lights.

And an eighth light beneath the water.

The dream supposedly occurs shortly before a Revenant dies.

No scientific explanation exists.

Some Revenants never experience it.

Others claim to have dreamed of the place repeatedly.

The Ashen Circle has documented the phenomenon but has never published a definitive explanation.

---

# 21. The Beastkin Moon Hunt

Different Beastkin cultures tell variations of a story about an ancient hunt beneath a red moon.

According to the oldest versions, the hunters followed a creature that could change its form.

After three days, they discovered that they had been following their own reflections.

The story is interpreted differently among Beastkin clans.

Some see it as a warning against pride.

Others see it as a story about identity.

Others claim it describes an actual supernatural creature that still exists.

---

# 22. The Returning Forest

One of the oldest stories associated with the future Sylvaneth lands describes a forest that disappeared during the Sundering.

The legend claims the forest was not destroyed.

Instead, it moved.

The trees supposedly carried their roots across the broken world and hid themselves until the continents were ready to reconnect.

Modern explorers have reported forests appearing in locations where no forests were previously recorded.

Whether these reports are related to the legend remains unknown.

---

# 23. The Black Star

Astronomical traditions across several cultures contain references to a black star.

It supposedly appeared shortly before the Sundering.

Some records describe it as:

* A dark star.
* A hole in the sky.
* A second moon.
* An eye.
* A shadow behind the stars.

Modern astronomers have found no evidence that such an object existed.

The Ashen Circle has nevertheless collected dozens of historical references to it.

The reason remains classified.

---

# 24. The World Beneath the World

One of the oldest myths claims that Elysium is not the only world.

According to the legend, another world exists beneath the surface.

It supposedly contains:

* Cities older than the gods.
* Forgotten civilizations.
* Creatures that have never seen sunlight.
* The original source of magic.
* The remains of civilizations that existed before the Age of Dawn.

Most scholars consider the story impossible.

Dwarven explorers are less dismissive.

Several unexplained underground structures have been discovered deep beneath the Ironpeak Holds.

---

# 25. Myths of the Gods

Each god has accumulated stories that may or may not reflect their actual nature.

### Solthar

Some legends claim Solthar once walked among humans disguised as a traveler.

### Nyxara

Some stories claim Nyxara knows every secret ever spoken beneath the moon.

### Terravox

Dwarven legends claim every mountain contains a sleeping fragment of Terravox.

### Maelithir

Sailors tell stories of Maelithir appearing during impossible storms.

### Ignareth

Some forge traditions claim the first flame was stolen from Ignareth by mortals.

### Voranthe

Some Revenant traditions claim Voranthe can see every possible ending of a mortal life.

### Aeloria

Druidic legends claim every living forest is connected through Aeloria.

None of these claims are confirmed.

---

# 26. The Myth of Mortal Ascension

A controversial collection of texts claims that mortals can eventually become gods.

According to the legend, the Elysian Circle were not always divine.

They were supposedly the first mortals to understand the fundamental laws of reality.

Most religions reject this idea.

Some scholars consider it dangerous.

Others believe it may explain why the gods valued mortal free will.

If true, the implications for Elysium would be enormous.

---

# 27. Myths of the Afterlife

Elysium has no universally accepted description of what happens after death.

Different cultures believe in different possibilities.

Some believe souls travel along the Quiet Road.

Some believe they return to nature.

Some believe they enter the halls of their ancestors.

Some believe the dead simply cease to exist.

Some Revenants believe death is not always permanent.

No living scholar has produced conclusive evidence for any afterlife.

This uncertainty is deliberately preserved.

---

# 28. The Myth of Reunification

A common Age of Reclamation belief claims that the continents will eventually reunite completely.

According to some versions, this will restore Elysium to its original form.

Others believe the continents will reconnect into something entirely new.

The most extreme version claims that when the world becomes whole again, the gods will return.

The opposing version claims that the reunion will trigger a second Sundering.

Nobody knows.

---

# 29. Contradictory Truths

Elysium's mythology deliberately contains contradictions.

Two legends may both appear impossible.

Two historical accounts may disagree.

Two cultures may claim ownership of the same hero.

A religious story may contradict archaeological evidence.

These contradictions should not always be resolved.

Some mysteries should remain mysteries.

The player should sometimes be left with several plausible explanations rather than one official answer.

---

# 30. Mythology as Gameplay

Mythology should not exist only in books.

Players can discover myths through:

* NPC dialogue
* Books
* Statues
* Ruins
* Paintings
* Songs
* Item descriptions
* Ancient inscriptions
* Questlines
* Environmental storytelling
* Hidden locations
* Archaeological discoveries
* Religious ceremonies

A player who pays attention should gradually build their own understanding of Elysium's past.

---

# 31. Myth-Based Secrets

Some myths may contain real clues.

For example:

```text id="5z2n9r"
Myth:
"The Nameless King sleeps beneath the mountain."

Player discovers:
An ancient royal seal beneath an Ironpeak ruin.

Possible conclusion:
The king may have actually existed.
```

The game should not immediately confirm the connection.

Players should be allowed to investigate.

This can create optional exploration chains and community theories.

---

# 32. Mythology and Factions

The Dawnbound Concord and Duskward Pact interpret mythology differently.

### Dawnbound Interpretation

Dawnbound scholars often emphasize myths that support:

* Civilization
* Cooperation
* Preservation
* Restoration
* Historical continuity

### Duskward Interpretation

Duskward scholars often emphasize myths that support:

* Independence
* Adaptation
* Survival
* Rejection of old institutions
* The dangers of repeating history

Neither side possesses a monopoly on historical truth.

Both factions have propaganda.

Both factions preserve genuine history.

Both factions have forgotten important information.

---

# 33. Mythology and the Player

The player should not be forced to accept any particular interpretation.

A player may believe:

* The gods still live.
* The gods are dead.
* Kaelgorath was misunderstood.
* The Sundering was inevitable.
* The continents are alive.
* The myths are mostly nonsense.
* The old civilizations were better.
* The old civilizations were fundamentally flawed.

These beliefs can be reflected through optional dialogue and roleplaying without changing the core story.

---

# 34. Future Revelations

Major mythology mysteries should be revealed gradually.

Potential long-term revelations include:

* The true origin of the gods.
* The actual cause of the Sundering.
* The identity of Kaelgorath's original domain.
* The reason the gods became silent.
* The origin of Revenants.
* The source of Sundering energy.
* The truth behind the Black Star.
* The existence or non-existence of a civilization before the Age of Dawn.
* The true nature of the drifting continents.
* Whether the gods can return.

These revelations should never invalidate earlier stories entirely.

Old myths should contain fragments of truth even when their conclusions are wrong.

---

# 35. Design Rules

1. Mythology should sit between history, religion, and folklore.
2. Myths may contain truth without being entirely true.
3. Different cultures should preserve different versions of the same story.
4. Political factions may deliberately alter myths.
5. Religious institutions may interpret myths through their own beliefs.
6. Ancient myths should not always have a definitive answer.
7. Major future revelations should be foreshadowed through mythology.
8. Myths should reward players who explore beyond the main story.
9. Mythology should never require players to understand every historical detail.
10. Contradictions are allowed and often intentional.
11. New lore should not casually overwrite established mythology.
12. Writers should distinguish between what characters believe and what the world actually knows.
13. Myths should be used to create curiosity rather than confusion.
14. Every major region should have its own local legends.
15. Some legends should eventually prove true.
16. Some legends should eventually prove false.
17. Some should remain unresolved permanently.
18. The player should never be certain that the oldest story is the most accurate one.

---

# 36. Core Principle

Elysium's mythology is built around a simple idea:

**The past was broken, and the truth broke with it.**

The people of Elysium inherited fragments.

A half-burned book.

A ruined temple.

A song with missing verses.

A name carved into stone.

A story told by an old soldier.

A relic whose purpose nobody remembers.

A legend that sounds impossible.

A legend that turns out to be true.

Every generation adds another layer.

The player does not simply learn Elysium's history.

They piece it together.

And sometimes, the pieces do not fit.

That is intentional.

Because somewhere beneath the myths, the propaganda, the religious interpretations, and the surviving records is the history of what actually happened to Elysium.

And some parts of that history are still waiting to be found.
