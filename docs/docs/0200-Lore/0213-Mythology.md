## 1. Overview

Mythology in Elysium sits between recorded history and pure legend. Many stories contain a kernel of truth about the Age of Dawn or the Age of Concord, but centuries of oral tradition, faction propaganda, and lost records have layered them with contradiction and mystery.

These myths are used to flavour dialogue, item lore, landmarks, and future expansion reveals.

---

## 2. Foundational Myths

### The Shaping of the World
The Elysian Circle sang Elysium into being from the void. Each god contributed a domain; together they seeded the founding races. Kaelgorath’s later resentment is sometimes framed as jealousy that mortals received free will while he remained bound to the Circle’s collective will.

### The First Sundering Prophecy
Scattered pre-Sundering texts allegedly warned that an eighth will would unmake the seven. Most scholars dismiss them as later forgeries; the Ashen Circle takes them seriously.

### The Silence of the Gods
After the Sundering the gods withdrew. Some myths claim they sleep, wounded. Others claim they watch in judgment. A minority of Duskward thinkers claim the gods were never truly divine — only powerful beings who fled when their creation turned against them.

### The Drifting Continents
Folk tales describe the continents as “children of the old world seeking one another again.” Cartographers treat the measurable reconnection of landmasses as a natural (if magical) process; poets prefer the family metaphor.

---

## 3. Regional Legends

Each region is encouraged to possess at least one local legend that is not required for the main story but rewards curious players (Pillar 1). Examples already seeded:

- The Weeping Hollow’s spectral duelists (Greywater Fens).
- The Sundered Spire frozen mid-collapse (Sunspire Hills).
- The Cairn of Names and the custom of adding commemorative names (Shattered Cairns).

---

## 4. Design Rules

- Myths may contradict one another; the world is allowed to be uncertain.
- No myth should hard-confirm or hard-deny major future plot points until the relevant expansion is ready.
- Writers should treat mythology as a living oral tradition that NPCs of different races and factions interpret differently.

---

## 5. Connection to Future Content

Many myths are deliberately incomplete so that expansions (especially Nightreach and the deeper Kaelgorath confrontations) can reveal older, more accurate versions.
