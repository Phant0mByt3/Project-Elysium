# 0208 — Side Stories

Optional questlines and character-driven narratives that exist alongside the main campaign.

Side stories expand Elysium's history, cultures, factions, kingdoms, religions, and characters without being required to complete the main story.

A player should be able to ignore most side stories and still understand the main campaign.

A player who completes them should understand Elysium on a much deeper level.

---

# Purpose

The main story follows the player's involvement in the Reconnection and the growing threat surrounding Kaelgorath.

Side stories focus on everything happening around that conflict.

They show how ordinary people experience the changing world.

A side story might be about:

* A family trying to survive a political dispute
* A soldier returning from war
* A scholar searching for a lost archive
* A dwarf clan fighting over an inheritance
* A Beastkin community defending its territory
* A Revenant attempting to find acceptance
* A merchant rebuilding an ancient trade route
* A priest questioning their faith
* A criminal organization exploiting the Reconnection
* A village discovering something buried beneath its foundations
* Two factions fighting over a seemingly insignificant piece of land

Not every story needs to involve gods, ancient magic, or Kaelgorath.

Some stories should simply be about people.

---

# Design Principles

## Complete Stories

Every side story should have a clear:

```text
Beginning
    ↓
Conflict
    ↓
Escalation
    ↓
Resolution
```

Side stories should not end simply because the player has reached the end of the available quests.

If a storyline is intended to continue in a future expansion, the current chapter should still have a satisfying conclusion.

---

## Character-Driven

Side stories should give secondary NPCs their own identities.

NPCs should have:

* Personal goals
* Relationships
* Motivations
* Flaws
* Conflicts
* Personal histories
* Character development

NPCs should not exist purely to give the player quests.

The player should feel like they are entering someone else's story.

---

## Regional Identity

Regional side stories should reflect the culture and problems of the region where they occur.

A quest in Solmere should not feel interchangeable with a quest in the Ashenclaw Tundra.

Regional stories should use:

* Local history
* Local factions
* Local architecture
* Local religions
* Local races
* Local politics
* Local environmental conditions
* Local conflicts

See [0102-Regions.md](../0100-World/0102-Regions.md).

---

# Types of Side Stories

## Personal Stories

Small-scale stories focused on individual NPCs.

Examples:

* Family conflicts
* Revenge
* Lost relationships
* Ambition
* Redemption
* Grief
* Betrayal
* Personal discovery

These stories should make the world feel inhabited.

---

## Regional Stories

Stories involving a specific settlement, territory, or local faction.

These can involve:

* Political disputes
* Bandit groups
* Monsters
* Resource shortages
* Territorial conflicts
* Local traditions
* Historical events

Regional stories should contribute to the identity of their region.

---

## Faction Stories

Stories involving the Dawnbound Concord, Duskward Pact, or their associated regional powers.

Faction side stories can reveal how ordinary members perceive the larger ideological conflict.

A Dawnbound quest should not automatically portray the Concord as correct.

A Duskward quest should not automatically portray the Pact as correct.

Both sides should contain good people, bad people, and people simply trying to survive.

---

## Kingdom Stories

Stories focused on political entities such as:

* The Concord Dominion
* Sylvaria Enclave
* Silverwatch
* Ironpeak Holds
* Ashenclaw Warbands

These stories explore how major political decisions affect ordinary communities.

---

## Religious Stories

Stories connected to the Elysian Circle and the religions surrounding them.

These stories can explore:

* Temples
* Priests
* Religious traditions
* Lost relics
* Miracles
* Religious disagreements
* Ancient rituals
* The silence of the gods

Not every religious story should provide definitive answers about the gods.

Some mysteries should remain mysteries.

---

## Historical Stories

Optional questlines that allow players to investigate the past.

These can involve:

* Age of Concord ruins
* Lost kingdoms
* Ancient documents
* Family histories
* Forgotten wars
* The Age of Ash
* The Sundering
* Aethercrest

Historical stories should sometimes present conflicting accounts.

Different cultures may remember the same event differently.

---

# Launch Side Stories

## The Miller's Debt

### Southern Shires

A low-level story following a Millhaven family struggling with debt.

The family borrowed money after a failed harvest.

The lender appears to be a legitimate merchant.

As the player investigates, they discover that the debt is being used to force local families off their land.

The player eventually discovers that the moneylender is secretly financing a local bandit organization.

The storyline can resolve in several ways.

The player can expose the operation.

They can help the family negotiate a legitimate settlement.

Or they can allow the family to leave the region and start again elsewhere.

The outcome affects the family's future but does not significantly alter the main story.

---

# Echoes of Aethercrest

### Cross-Continent | Levels 20–45

An optional storyline following a researcher from the **Cartographer's League**.

The researcher believes the surviving historical records of Aethercrest are incomplete.

The player helps recover fragments of maps, documents, and artifacts scattered between Aurelia and Vethmoor.

The investigation gradually reveals that several historical records contradict one another.

The researcher eventually discovers evidence that Aethercrest's final moments were deliberately misrepresented in later historical accounts.

The story concludes shortly before the main campaign reaches the Sunken Concord.

Players who complete the chain enter the launch raid with a much deeper understanding of the ruins.

The questline does not reveal everything.

Some discoveries remain unexplained.

---

# Clanbreaker

### Ashenclaw Tundra | Duskward

A disgraced orc warrior has been exiled from their clan.

They claim they were betrayed by their own Warcouncil.

The player helps them investigate the circumstances surrounding their exile.

The investigation reveals that the warrior did break clan law, but the punishment was manipulated by another clan member attempting to gain political influence.

The player eventually helps expose the manipulation.

The warrior must then decide whether to return to their clan or establish a new path.

The story explores:

* Orc clan traditions
* The Ashenclaw Warbands
* The Warcouncil
* Duskward philosophy
* Honor
* Family
* Independence

---

# The Last Forgemaster

### Ironpeak Holds

A dwarven clan discovers that its ancestral forge has begun producing weapons without anyone operating it.

The clan believes Terravox has blessed the forge.

The player investigates.

The truth is stranger.

The forge is powered by an ancient mechanism built during the Age of Concord.

The clan must decide whether to preserve the old technology, destroy it, or use it to rebuild their declining industry.

The storyline explores the tension between dwarven tradition and technological recovery.

---

# The Silent Shrine

### Aurelia

A small shrine dedicated to Aeloria has stopped receiving pilgrims.

The shrine's keeper believes the goddess has abandoned them.

The player discovers that the shrine itself has been damaged by ancient corruption.

Repairing it causes a strange phenomenon.

For a brief moment, the shrine produces what appears to be a genuine divine response.

The event cannot be explained.

The player receives no direct message from Aeloria.

The story ends with the shrine keeper choosing to continue their faith despite not knowing whether the gods are listening.

---

# Ash Without a Grave

### Revenant Territories

A Revenant community discovers an old burial site containing dozens of bodies dating from the early Age of Ash.

The bodies are not undead.

But several of them have begun moving.

The player investigates the site and discovers that the dead are responding to residual Sundering energy.

The story focuses on the Revenants' relationship with death.

The player eventually has to decide whether the remains should be destroyed, preserved, or relocated.

There is no perfect answer.

---

# Silverwatch

### Aurelia

A Silverwatch commander discovers that several villages within their territory have stopped paying taxes.

The initial assumption is rebellion.

The truth is more complicated.

The villages have been paying their taxes to another local authority because Silverwatch has been unable to protect them from bandits.

The player investigates the situation and uncovers corruption within the regional administration.

The storyline explores the difference between political allegiance and local loyalty.

The player can strengthen Silverwatch's authority, expose corrupt officials, or help establish a more independent local administration.

---

# The Cartographer's Mistake

### Cross-Region

A Cartographer's League expedition discovers a location that does not appear on any surviving map.

The team initially believes it is a newly exposed ruin.

The player helps document it.

Eventually they discover that the location appears on several extremely old maps.

The location was deliberately removed from later records.

The reason for its removal becomes the focus of the story.

The conclusion reveals a piece of forgotten history but leaves the larger mystery unresolved.

---

# The Ashen Circle

### Cross-Continent

A scholar from the Ashen Circle disappears while investigating a corrupted ruin.

The player is sent to locate them.

The investigation reveals that the scholar discovered evidence of multiple Kaelgorath fragments.

The player eventually finds them alive.

They refuse to leave.

They believe the ruins contain information that could explain why the fragments are awakening.

The player can help retrieve the research or assist the scholar in continuing the investigation.

The story provides additional context for the main campaign without revealing its major discoveries.

## Independent Adventurers

The player can encounter other adventurers pursuing their own goals.

Some may become recurring characters.

They might:

# Class Stories

Each class should eventually have optional stories connected to its traditions.

These stories should explore what it actually means to follow that class in Elysium.

For example:

### Vanguard

A veteran attempts to train a new generation of soldiers after losing faith in traditional military leadership.

### Arcanist

A mage discovers a spell that disappeared during the Sundering.

### Shade

A covert operative discovers that their organization has been infiltrated.

### Warden

A protector discovers that a forest is responding to an ancient magical disturbance.

### Oathkeeper

A knight must decide whether an oath made centuries ago is still worth keeping.

### Wayfarer

An explorer discovers an ancient road that should not exist.

Class stories should not be required to unlock or play the class.

# Side Story Rewards

Side stories should reward players with more than equipment.

Possible rewards include:

* Titles
* Cosmetics
* Mounts
* Pets
* Housing items
* Weapons
* Armor appearances
* Emotes
* Lore entries
* Reputation
* Faction standing
* NPC relationships
* Achievement progress
* Access to optional areas
* Unique dialogue

Some rewards should be purely cosmetic.

The best side stories should be worth completing even when the player does not need the reward.

---

# Consequences

Side stories may change small parts of the world.

Examples include:

* A village gaining new NPCs
* A shop opening
* A destroyed building being repaired
* A faction gaining influence
* A character moving to another city
* A road becoming safer
* A new merchant appearing
* A shrine being restored
* A regional NPC changing dialogue

These consequences should make the player's actions feel persistent without requiring the entire world to branch into separate versions.

---

# Side Story Philosophy

Side stories should answer a simple question:

**"What is happening to everyone else while the main story is happening?"**

The main campaign deals with Elysium's largest threats.

Side stories show how those threats affect ordinary people.

A farmer does not care about the fate of Kaelgorath.

A merchant cares about whether a road is safe.

A soldier cares about whether they will survive the next border conflict.

A priest cares about whether their god is listening.

A child cares about whether their family can afford food.

A Revenant cares about whether people will stop treating them like monsters.

These stories make Elysium feel like a world rather than a sequence of main quests.

---

# Writing Rules

Side stories must:

1. Have a complete narrative arc.
2. Give their main NPCs clear motivations.
3. Reflect the region and culture where they occur.
4. Avoid contradicting established history.
5. Avoid making every conflict secretly about Kaelgorath.
6. Avoid making every NPC connected to the main story.
7. Allow morally complicated outcomes where appropriate.
8. Provide meaningful rewards or consequences.
9. Remain optional.
10. Make the world feel alive independently of the player.

The main story explains what is happening to Elysium.

Side stories explain what is happening to its people.
