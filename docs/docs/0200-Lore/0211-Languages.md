# 1. Overview

Languages in Elysium exist primarily for culture, flavour, immersion, environmental storytelling, and occasional puzzles.

They are not intended to become a restrictive gameplay system.

Players should not need to spend hours learning a language before they can understand an important quest.

Instead, language differences should make Elysium feel like a world inhabited by different peoples with their own histories and identities.

Languages can appear in:

* NPC dialogue
* Names
* Titles
* Place names
* Item names
* Books
* Inscriptions
* Religious texts
* Ancient ruins
* Faction terminology
* Clan traditions
* Songs and ceremonies
* Historical records
* Environmental storytelling

Common remains the primary language used between different cultures.

---

# 2. Common

**Speakers:** All playable races
**Primary Regions:** All major civilized regions
**Status:** Modern universal language

Common is the primary trade and diplomatic language of modern Elysium.

Most humans, High Elves, dwarves, orcs, Beastkin, and Revenants can communicate in Common.

It developed from several older languages and became increasingly widespread during the later Age of Concord.

Following the Sundering, Common became even more important because isolated civilizations needed a shared language when reconnecting.

Common is therefore the primary language used by:

* Merchants
* Adventurers
* Diplomats
* Soldiers
* Travelers
* Major factions
* Cross-cultural settlements

Common does not mean that all cultures speak identically.

Regional accents, expressions, slang, and vocabulary should remain common.

---

# 3. Aurelian

**Speakers:** Humans of Aurelia
**Primary Region:** Aurelia
**Associated Culture:** Concord Dominion
**Status:** Modern regional language and cultural dialect

Aurelian is the primary traditional human language of Aurelia.

Modern Aurelian developed from the human dialects spoken during the later Age of Concord.

It is closely related to Common.

Most Aurelian speakers can switch between the two without difficulty.

Aurelian is particularly common in:

* Government
* Nobility
* Military commands
* Legal documents
* Religious ceremonies
* Traditional songs
* Historical records

Formal Aurelian is associated with the Concord Dominion and the Dawnbound tradition.

Regional communities may use considerably different accents and vocabulary.

---

# 4. High Elven

**Speakers:** High Elves
**Primary Region:** Sylvaria and High Elven communities
**Associated Culture:** Sylvaria Enclave
**Status:** Living language

High Elven is an old language that survived the Sundering relatively intact.

It is known for its precise vocabulary and extensive terminology surrounding:

* Magic
* Astronomy
* Philosophy
* History
* Nature
* Religion

Many High Elven magical texts have no exact Common translation.

This makes High Elven particularly important to scholars and mages.

The language also contains many words dating back to the Age of Concord.

High Elven speakers often distinguish between modern High Elven and the older scholarly forms used in ancient texts.

---

# 5. Dwarven

**Speakers:** Dwarves
**Primary Region:** Ironpeak Holds
**Associated Culture:** Dwarven clans
**Status:** Living language

Dwarven is the primary language of the Ironpeak Holds.

It is strongly associated with:

* Stone
* Metalworking
* Engineering
* Mining
* Clan identity
* Ancestry

Dwarven writing uses distinctive runic forms.

Many dwarven names, forge inscriptions, weapons, and clan monuments retain their original Dwarven terminology even when translated into Common.

Different Ironpeak clans have regional dialects.

Some dialect differences are large enough that two clans may use Common when discussing technical matters.

---

# 6. Orcish

**Speakers:** Orcs
**Primary Region:** Ashenclaw Tundra
**Associated Culture:** Ashenclaw Warbands
**Status:** Living language

Orcish is spoken throughout the Ashenclaw Warbands.

The language is generally direct and places significant emphasis on:

* Clan
* Family
* Honor
* Combat
* Territory
* Ancestry

There is no single standardized Orcish dialect.

Individual clans have their own variations.

Some clan words have no direct Common equivalent because they describe specific cultural concepts.

Orcish is commonly used during:

* Clan ceremonies
* War councils
* Oaths
* Challenges
* Funerals
* Traditional songs

---

# 7. Beastongue

**Speakers:** Beastkin
**Primary Regions:** Aurelia, Vethmoor, future Kharzul Wastes
**Status:** Language family

Beastongue is not a single standardized language.

It is a family of related languages and dialects spoken by different Beastkin cultures.

Different clans may have completely different forms of:

* Pronunciation
* Writing
* Grammar
* Vocabulary
* Naming conventions

Some Beastkin communities have adopted Common heavily.

Others retain their traditional languages almost entirely.

The languages of the future Kharzul Wastes will represent much older and more developed branches of the Beastongue family.

Beastongue should therefore never be treated as one universal Beastkin language.

---

# 8. Revenant Cant

**Speakers:** Revenants
**Primary Regions:** Revenant communities throughout Elysium
**Status:** Cultural language

Revenant Cant developed among Revenants during the Age of Ash.

It is not an ancient language.

It emerged as Revenant communities developed their own traditions and forms of communication.

The language is heavily influenced by older human and regional dialects but contains terminology associated with:

* The Sundering
* Death
* Memory
* The passage of time
* Revenant identity
* Sundering energy

Its speech is often described as measured and deliberate.

Some older expressions have unusual meanings because their original context has been forgotten.

Revenant Cant is rarely taught outside Revenant communities.

---

# 9. Old Concord

**Speakers:** Historical scholars, High Elves, specialized researchers
**Origin:** Age of Concord
**Status:** Ancient language

Old Concord is the formal written language of the late Age of Concord.

It is no longer spoken as an ordinary language.

It survives primarily through:

* Ancient inscriptions
* Government records
* Magical texts
* Aethercrest ruins
* Religious documents
* Legendary equipment
* Ancient monuments

Old Concord is one of the most important languages for historical research.

Some magical systems were designed around its grammar and terminology.

As a result, translating an Old Concord spell literally into Common can sometimes remove important meaning.

---

# 10. Religious Languages

The Elysian Circle does not possess one universal divine language.

Each religion developed its own terminology and ceremonial vocabulary.

Most religious texts are written in the language of their original culture.

Examples include:

### Solthar

A formal vocabulary associated with:

* Light
* Justice
* Dawn
* Renewal

### Nyxara

Uses symbolic terminology associated with:

* Night
* Secrets
* Silence
* The Moon

### Terravox

Uses traditional Dwarven terminology relating to:

* Stone
* Endurance
* Craft
* Ancestry

### Maelithir

Uses maritime terminology and older coastal dialects.

### Ignareth

Uses Orcish and Dwarven terminology relating to:

* Fire
* War
* Forging
* Creation

### Voranthe

Uses terms associated with:

* Death
* Fate
* Memory
* Passage

### Aeloria

Uses High Elven and regional terminology relating to:

* Life
* Growth
* Nature
* Renewal

Religious terminology should not require players to learn another language.

# 11. Faction Terminology

The Dawnbound Concord and Duskward Pact do not have separate languages.

They are ideological alliances rather than independent cultures.

Both factions use the languages of the people who belong to them.

Their terminology, slang, and political vocabulary can still differ.

For example, a Dawnbound speaker may refer to the Sundering as:

> "the great catastrophe"

while a Duskward speaker might call it:

> "the breaking."

These differences should communicate ideology without creating artificial language barriers.

# 12. Historical Language Development

Languages changed significantly throughout Elysium's history.

```text id="n7f4c2"
Age of Dawn
      │
      ▼
Early Mortal Languages
      │
      ▼
Age of Concord
      │
      ├── Old Concord
      ├── Early Aurelian
      ├── Early High Elven
      └── Early Dwarven / Orcish
      │
      ▼
The Sundering
      │
      ▼
Age of Ash
      │
      ├── Regional dialects develop
      ├── Common fragments
      └── Revenant Cant emerges
      │
      ▼
Age of Reclamation
      │
      └── Languages begin interacting again
```

The Reconnection is therefore likely to cause significant linguistic exchange.

New words, slang, and terminology can spread between continents.

# 13. Language in Dialogue

The Dialogue System should support occasional untranslated words.

Example:

```text
Orc:

"That is not your war, human.
Go home before the clan says dorak."

[Context suggests that "dorak" means something
similar to "outsider" or "trespasser."]
```

The player should understand the general meaning through context.

Important information should still be translated.

---

# 14. Language-Based Dialogue Conditions

Language knowledge can occasionally unlock additional dialogue.

Examples:

```text
[Requires High Elven]

"You recognize the inscription."
```

Or:

```text
[Requires Dwarven Cultural Knowledge]

"You understand the clan's ceremonial phrase."
```

These should provide additional information rather than block the critical path.

---

# 15. Language and Professions

Certain professions or reputation groups may provide better access to ancient languages.

Examples:

* Cartographer's League
* Scholars
* Archaeologists
* Historians
* Mages
* Religious orders

A player might discover an inscription and initially receive:

> "You cannot understand this writing."

After gaining the appropriate knowledge:

> "This is Old Concord. The inscription describes a sealed passage beneath the city."

This creates optional discovery without forcing every player into a language system.

---

# 16. Language Puzzles

Some optional puzzles may use language.

Examples include:

* Translating an inscription
* Matching ancient symbols
* Understanding a ceremonial phrase
* Deciphering a damaged map
* Identifying a forgotten place name
* Reconstructing a broken sentence

These puzzles should primarily reward:

* Lore
* Exploration
* Cosmetics
* Achievements
* Hidden areas
* Optional rewards

They should not block the main campaign.

---

# 17. Names and Place Names

Language should influence naming.

A place's name should provide subtle information about its history.

For example:

* Aurelian settlements may have human-derived names.
* Ironpeak locations may use Dwarven names.
* Ashenclaw settlements may retain Orcish names.
* Ancient ruins may use Old Concord names.
* Beastkin territories may use names from local Beastongue dialects.

Some locations may have multiple names.

Example:

```text
Modern Common:
The Sunken Capital

Aurelian:
Aethercrest

Old Concord:
[ancient form]

Orcish:
[traditional name]
```

Different NPCs may use different names for the same place.

---

# 18. Language and Lore Discovery

Language should help reinforce one of Elysium's major themes:

**History is fragmented.**

Two cultures may describe the same event differently.

An ancient inscription may contradict a modern textbook.

A religious text may describe the Sundering differently from a military record.

The player should sometimes need to compare multiple sources to understand what actually happened.

---

# 19. Future Languages

New languages should be introduced when new cultures and continents become important.

Potential future languages include:

### Sylvaneth Languages

Ancient woodland languages associated with the Sylvaneth peoples.

### Nightreach Tongues

Corrupted languages influenced by the supernatural conditions of Nightreach.

### Kharzul Dialects

Additional Beastongue branches and entirely new languages belonging to the cultures of Kharzul Wastes.

### Ancient or Divine Languages

If future lore establishes civilizations or entities predating the known mortal cultures, their languages should be documented here rather than retroactively forcing them into an existing language.

---

# 20. Design Rules

1. Common should remain the universal practical language.
2. Language should enrich the world, not frustrate players.
3. Main-story quests must never require language knowledge.
4. Critical quest information must always be understandable.
5. Different cultures should have distinct linguistic identities.
6. Beastkin languages should remain diverse rather than becoming one universal language.
7. The Dawnbound Concord and Duskward Pact should not have separate languages.
8. Ancient languages should be used heavily for environmental storytelling.
9. Language differences should sometimes reveal cultural differences.
10. Language should never be used as an excuse for NPCs to behave unrealistically.
11. New languages should only be added when supported by actual cultures or historical groups.
12. Translations should preserve cultural meaning rather than simply replacing every word with Common.
13. Important NPC dialogue should remain readable even when regional language is used.
14. Language-related discoveries should primarily reward exploration and curiosity.
15. Elysium's languages should evolve naturally alongside its history.

---

# 21. Core Principle

Languages should make Elysium feel culturally fragmented without making it mechanically frustrating.

The player should be able to walk through Solmere, enter an Ironpeak settlement, visit an Ashenclaw camp, and eventually reach the Beastkin cultures of Kharzul Wastes and immediately feel that these peoples have different histories.

They may all understand Common.

They should not all sound the same.

The languages of Elysium are another way of showing that the world survived the Sundering differently in different places.
