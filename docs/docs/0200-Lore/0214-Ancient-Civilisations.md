# 1. Overview

Ancient civilisations are the cultures, kingdoms, empires, cities, and societies that existed before and during the earliest periods of recorded Elysian history.

Their remains are scattered throughout the world.

Some became the foundations of modern kingdoms.

Some were destroyed during the Sundering.

Some disappeared long before the Sundering.

Others may have never been recorded by modern historians at all.

Their ruins are among the most important sources of information about Elysium's past.

They are also among its greatest sources of mystery.

Ancient civilisation content should make the world feel old.

A ruined city should not feel like a dungeon that was created specifically for the player.

It should feel like a real place that existed for centuries before the player ever arrived.

---

# 2. The Layers of Elysian History

Ancient sites generally contain several historical layers.

```text
Ancient Era
     ↓
Pre-Concord Civilisations
     ↓
Age of Concord
     ↓
Sundering
     ↓
Age of Ash
     ↓
Modern Elysium
```

A single location may contain evidence from multiple periods.

An ancient temple may have been built by a Pre-Concord civilisation.

The Concord may have expanded it into a larger sanctuary.

The Sundering may have destroyed part of it.

Survivors may have converted the ruins into a settlement during the Age of Ash.

The modern population may now live on top of all four layers without fully understanding what lies beneath them.

This layering is an important part of Elysium's environmental storytelling.

---

# 3. The Concord Civilisation

The Concord Civilisation was the dominant multi-racial civilisation of the Age of Concord.

It was not a single empire.

Instead, it was a massive network of kingdoms, cities, guilds, academic institutions, trade routes, and political alliances that shared a common cultural framework.

Humans, High Elves, and Dwarves formed its largest populations, although other peoples lived within its borders.

The Concord period produced:

* Aethercrest
* Major magical academies
* Intercontinental roads
* Large-scale aqueduct systems
* Arcane transportation networks
* Monumental temples
* Ancient libraries
* Advanced forging techniques
* Magical communication systems
* Large defensive fortifications

Much of modern Elysian civilisation is built on surviving Concord foundations.

---

# 4. Aethercrest

Aethercrest was the greatest city of the Age of Concord.

It was simultaneously:

* Political capital
* Religious centre
* Academic centre
* Trade hub
* Magical research centre
* Cultural meeting point

The city was built where several major roads and magical ley lines converged.

Its central district contained the **Concordium**, a monumental complex where representatives of different races and kingdoms gathered.

The city also contained the **Grand Archivum**, believed to have housed millions of records.

Almost all of it was destroyed during the Sundering.

The ruins beneath the Shattered Cairns are among the most important archaeological sites in Elysium.

---

# 5. The Pre-Concord Kingdoms

Before the Age of Concord, Elysium was divided into numerous independent kingdoms and cultures.

Very little survives from this period.

Most modern historians divide the Pre-Concord era into broad cultural groups rather than named nations.

This is largely because surviving records are fragmented.

Some ruins contain inscriptions written in languages that modern scholars cannot completely translate.

Several kingdoms mentioned in surviving Concord documents have never been located.

Others may have existed only as political myths.

---

# 6. The First Human Kingdoms

Human civilisation developed rapidly during the centuries preceding the Age of Concord.

Early human kingdoms were generally smaller than their later descendants.

They relied heavily on:

* Agriculture
* River trade
* Stone fortifications
* Mounted warfare
* Early metallurgy

Many modern Aurelian settlements were built around former human fortresses.

Some of the oldest human ruins contain architectural styles that completely disappeared during the Concord period.

---

# 7. The First Elven Realms

The earliest High Elven societies were heavily focused on knowledge, astronomy, magic, and religion.

Their cities were frequently constructed around enormous observatories and arcane towers.

Many early Elven ruins contain celestial maps that appear to show stars that no longer exist.

Whether these represent different constellations, magical phenomena, or something else remains unknown.

---

# 8. The Forge Clans of Terravox

The ancestors of the modern Dwarves lived deep beneath the mountains.

The oldest known Dwarven settlements were not large cities.

They were interconnected underground communities built around mines, workshops, temples, and defensive gates.

The **Forge Clans of Terravox** eventually became the cultural ancestors of the Ironpeak Holds.

Their greatest surviving achievements include:

* Deep-forged weapons
* Ancient runes
* Reinforced mountain gates
* Mechanical systems
* Underground water networks
* Heat-powered forges
* Stone engineering

Some of their deepest structures have never been fully explored.

---

# 9. The Star-Singers

The Star-Singers were an early High Elven scholarly tradition.

They believed that the movement of celestial bodies reflected the will of the gods.

Their greatest achievement was supposedly the **Celestial Archive**, a collection of astronomical observations spanning centuries.

The original archive has never been found.

Fragments of Star-Singer texts have been recovered across Aurelia and Vethmoor.

Several surviving fragments contain references to events that supposedly occurred before the Age of Concord.

Some scholars believe the Star-Singers may have recorded evidence of civilisations that existed before the known founding races.

---

# 10. The Beastkin Ancestors

The ancestors of the Beastkin lived across enormous territories that are now part of Vethmoor and the Kharzul Wastes.

Unlike the urban societies of the Concord, many Beastkin cultures were nomadic.

They built temporary settlements, stone circles, seasonal cities, and underground shelters.

Some later developed permanent cities.

These cultures left behind:

* Totemic monuments
* Buried settlements
* Hunting shrines
* Ancient roads
* Stone circles
* Astronomical structures
* Clan burial grounds

Many of their oldest sites remain buried beneath the Kharzul Wastes.

---

# 11. The First Orc Clans

The ancestors of the modern Orc clans developed around harsh northern environments.

Their early societies were heavily clan-based.

Strength, loyalty, survival, and ancestral memory were central to their culture.

Unlike the later Ashenclaw Warbands, these clans were not united.

Many ancient Orc settlements were destroyed during conflicts between clans.

Some surviving oral traditions claim that the clans eventually learned that constant warfare would destroy them.

Others claim the first true Orc alliance was created only after an external enemy threatened them.

---

# 12. The Ancient Revenant Question

The origin of the Revenants remains one of Elysium's greatest historical mysteries.

Modern records place the first confirmed Revenant centuries after the Sundering.

However, several ancient sites contain unexplained references to people who:

* Did not age normally.
* Could survive injuries that should be fatal.
* Were described as unnaturally cold.
* Could perceive lingering magical energy.
* Were feared by other races.

These references predate the currently accepted origin of the Revenants.

Most historians believe the descriptions refer to something else.

The Ashen Circle is not convinced.

---

# 13. Civilisations Lost Before the Concord

Not every ancient civilisation was destroyed by the Sundering.

Some disappeared thousands of years earlier.

Evidence of these cultures exists in isolated locations.

Their ruins often contain:

* Unknown languages
* Unfamiliar architecture
* Strange metals
* Impossible measurements
* Unknown religious symbols
* Technology not associated with any known civilisation

These sites are sometimes called **Pre-Concord Anomalies**.

Only a handful have been properly studied.

---

# 14. The Sundered Remnants

The Sundered Remnants were not a single civilisation.

The term refers to populations caught directly within areas affected by the strongest Sundering energy.

Some survivors changed physically.

Others developed unusual magical abilities.

Some settlements became isolated for generations.

Others disappeared completely.

Several modern myths about monsters and cursed peoples may actually originate from these communities.

The exact relationship between the Sundered Remnants and the Revenants remains unknown.

---

# 15. The Ashen Age Settlements

After the Sundering, survivors often moved into abandoned ancient structures.

A palace became a fortress.

A temple became a shelter.

A library became a storage building.

A magical academy became a town hall.

This created some of the strangest archaeological sites in Elysium.

A modern village may unknowingly be built directly on top of a civilisation thousands of years older.

---

# 16. The Lost Kingdoms

Ancient records contain the names of hundreds of kingdoms that no longer exist.

Some disappeared during the Sundering.

Others vanished before it.

Known examples include:

* Vael Taryn
* Orenth
* Cael Vora
* Tharuun
* Elaris
* Morvane
* Kethra
* Avaron

Little is known about most of them.

Their names appear on maps, inscriptions, royal records, and ancient trade documents.

Some have no surviving ruins.

Others have ruins but no confirmed location.

---

# 17. The Ancient Roads

One of the most important discoveries of the Age of Reclamation has been the rediscovery of ancient road networks.

Some roads were constructed during the Concord.

Others appear significantly older.

Certain roads connect locations that have no known historical relationship.

This has led some scholars to believe that a major civilisation existed before the Concord and controlled much larger portions of Elysium than previously believed.

The Cartographer's League considers this one of its greatest ongoing research projects.

---

# 18. Ancient Magical Infrastructure

Ancient civilisations were capable of large-scale magical engineering.

Examples include:

* Leyline stabilisers
* Teleportation gates
* Arcane lighting
* Magical water systems
* Defensive barriers
* Weather control devices
* Communication crystals
* Enchanted transportation systems

Most stopped functioning during the Sundering.

Some have recently begun activating again.

This is one of the main reasons ancient ruins have become increasingly dangerous during the Age of Reclamation.

---

# 19. The Ancient Archivarium

The **Ancient Archivarium** is one of the greatest mysteries in Elysium.

It is believed to be a strange dimension containing knowledge gathered from civilisations across history.

It does not appear to exist in a conventional physical location.

Instead, entrances to it can occasionally be found throughout Elysium.

These entrances may appear as:

* Ancient doors
* Broken portals
* Hidden chambers
* Mirrors
* Stone arches
* Magical circles
* Ruined libraries
* Objects that should not function

No two entrances appear identical.

---

# 20. The Nature of the Archivarium

Nobody knows who created the Ancient Archivarium.

Some believe it was constructed by the gods.

Others believe it was built by an ancient mortal civilisation.

The Ashen Circle believes it may predate the Elysian Circle entirely.

Inside, the laws of space and time do not always behave normally.

A doorway may lead to a library that appears to have been abandoned yesterday.

A staircase may descend for hours while the player only moves a few metres in the outside world.

A room may contain records from events that have not happened yet.

These properties make the Archivarium extremely dangerous to study.

---

# 21. The Archivarium's Collections

The Archivarium supposedly contains:

* Historical records
* Lost languages
* Ancient maps
* Royal archives
* Religious texts
* Scientific discoveries
* Forbidden magical research
* Records of extinct civilisations
* Prophecies
* Personal journals
* Lost technologies
* Records of the Sundering
* Information about Kaelgorath

Not every record is accurate.

Some may have been intentionally altered.

Some may describe events from entirely different civilisations.

Some may contradict known history.

---

# 22. Archivarium Portals

Ancient Archivarium portals are intentionally rare.

Players should discover them through exploration rather than being directed to them immediately.

A portal may be hidden behind:

* A removable wall section.
* A ruined statue.
* An ancient inscription.
* A secret lever.
* A damaged magical device.
* A seemingly useless object.

This supports Elysium's exploration philosophy.

A location that appears meaningless may contain an entrance to one of the most important places in the world.

---

# 23. The Archivarium as a World System

The Archivarium can function as more than a lore location.

It can contain:

* Lore challenges
* Puzzle rooms
* Historical simulations
* Lost civilisation scenarios
* Rare collectibles
* Ancient crafting recipes
* Unique cosmetic items
* Secret questlines
* Hidden bosses
* World mysteries

Some sections may change depending on the player's progression.

---

# 24. Historical Simulations

Certain Archivarium chambers may recreate moments from ancient history.

The player might witness:

* The founding of Aethercrest.
* The construction of an ancient city.
* The first meeting between races.
* A Pre-Concord war.
* The final moments before the Sundering.

These should not necessarily be perfect recreations.

The Archivarium may only possess fragments of the original information.

A simulation could therefore contain missing people, corrupted locations, or contradictory events.

---

# 25. The Forbidden Archive

Deep within the Archivarium lies a section known only as the **Forbidden Archive**.

Very few records mention it.

Those that do describe it differently.

Some claim it contains knowledge that the gods themselves wanted forgotten.

Others claim it contains the complete history of Kaelgorath.

The Ashen Circle has spent generations attempting to locate it.

No confirmed entrance has been discovered.

---

# 26. The Archivarium and Kaelgorath

The Archivarium may contain information about Kaelgorath that cannot be found anywhere else.

This does not necessarily mean the records are trustworthy.

Some may have been written by Kaelgorath himself.

Others may have been written by his enemies.

A central long-term mystery is determining whether the surviving accounts of Kaelgorath describe a genuine villain, a misunderstood figure, or something far more complicated.

The truth should be revealed gradually through later content.

---

# 27. The Civilisation Before the Gods

Several discoveries suggest that Elysium may have been inhabited before the Age of Dawn.

This possibility is considered extremely controversial.

Evidence includes:

* Structures older than known divine settlements.
* Symbols that appear in unrelated ancient cultures.
* Materials not used by any known civilisation.
* Maps showing unknown continents.
* Objects that appear to predate known magic.

If true, this would completely change the accepted history of Elysium.

The question is not simply:

**"Who lived before the gods?"**

It is:

**"Why did nobody remember them?"**

---

# 28. Ancient Civilisation Classification

The Cartographer's League currently uses the following classification system.

### Class I — Known

The civilisation is historically confirmed and sufficiently documented.

### Class II — Fragmentary

The civilisation is confirmed, but significant information is missing.

### Class III — Legendary

The civilisation is mentioned in myths but has not been confirmed archaeologically.

### Class IV — Anomalous

Physical evidence exists, but its origin cannot be determined.

### Class V — Impossible

Evidence appears to contradict established history or physical laws.

Class V sites are extremely rare.

The Ashen Circle has requested that all Class V discoveries be reported directly to them.

---

# 29. Ancient Civilisation Loot

Ancient objects should not always be generic treasure.

Players may discover:

* Broken royal seals
* Ancient coins
* Ritual weapons
* Lost crafting tools
* Historical documents
* Magical fragments
* Ancient maps
* Archaeological artefacts
* Decorative relics
* Unidentified technology

Some items may initially appear useless.

Later discoveries may reveal their significance.

# 30. Ancient Civilisations and Professions

Ancient civilisation content connects heavily with exploration and professions.

Potential profession interactions include:

### Archaeology

Excavating ruins and identifying artefacts.

### Blacksmithing

Recreating ancient forging techniques.

### Enchanting

Studying old magical inscriptions.

### Cartography

Reconstructing ancient maps.

### Alchemy

Identifying ancient substances and recipes.

### Jewelcrafting

Restoring ancient gemstones and relics.

These systems allow players to interact with history rather than simply reading about it.

---

# 31. Ancient Civilisations and Factions

Different factions approach ancient history differently.

### Dawnbound Concord

Generally believes ancient knowledge should be preserved and used to rebuild civilisation.

Some Dawnbound scholars want to restore lost Concord technology.

Others fear that repeating the past will recreate the conditions that caused the Sundering.

### Duskward Pact

Generally believes ancient knowledge should be approached cautiously.

Some Pact members argue that the old world failed precisely because it became too dependent on ancient institutions and magic.

Others actively seek ancient weapons and technologies to ensure Elysium is never again powerless.

### The Cartographer's League

Attempts to remain politically neutral.

Its priority is documenting what actually exists.

### The Ashen Circle

Focuses heavily on forbidden knowledge, Kaelgorath, Sundering energy, and unexplained ancient phenomena.

Its members are willing to investigate places that other organisations consider too dangerous.

---

# 32. Environmental Storytelling

Ancient civilisations should be visible in the environment.

Players should be able to recognise historical periods through:

* Architecture
* Building materials
* Road design
* Symbols
* Writing systems
* Weapon design
* Burial customs
* Religious imagery
* Magical technology

The player should eventually be able to walk into a ruin and think:

**"This doesn't look like Concord architecture."**

That recognition should come from consistent world design rather than exposition.

---

# 33. Civilisation Recognition

A major long-term goal is allowing players to identify ancient cultures without needing a quest marker.

For example:

```text
Player sees:

Black stone walls
Circular windows
Star symbols
Silver inlays
Unusual astronomical carvings

Player learns:

Star-Singer architecture
```

Later, the player may encounter the same symbols hundreds of hours later.

They immediately recognise the connection.

This creates a sense that the world has a coherent history.

---

# 34. The Rule of Forgotten Knowledge

Ancient civilisations should never be portrayed as simply "better" than modern Elysium.

They may have achieved incredible things.

They may also have made terrible mistakes.

Ancient technology can be:

* Powerful
* Dangerous
* Inefficient
* Incomplete
* Misunderstood
* Corrupted
* Better suited to ancient conditions

Modern people should not automatically understand ancient technology just because they discover it.

---

# 35. The Rule of Ruins

Every major ruin should answer at least one question and create another.

Example:

```text
Question:
Who built this city?

Answer:
The Star-Singers.

New question:
Why does the city contain symbols belonging to a civilisation
that supposedly existed thousands of years earlier?
```

This prevents ancient locations from becoming simple lore dumps.

---

# 36. Ancient Civilisations and Future Expansions

Each future continent should reveal older layers of Elysian history.

### Sylvaneth

May reveal forgotten Elven cultures and ancient forests that existed before the Concord.

### Kharzul Wastes

May reveal lost Beastkin cities and evidence of civilisations predating the known races.

### Nightreach

May reveal the deepest consequences of the Sundering and the origins of the Sundered Remnants.

Future expansions should make the world feel older, not simply larger.

---

# 37. Long-Term Mystery

The oldest civilisation in Elysium may not be the Concord.

It may not even be one of the known races.

Scattered evidence suggests that something existed before the Age of Dawn.

Something that may have understood magic differently.

Something that may have known about the gods before the gods shaped the world.

Something whose ruins may still exist.

The Ancient Archivarium may contain the only surviving records.

Whether this civilisation truly existed remains one of Elysium's deepest unanswered questions.

---

# 38. Design Rules

1. Ancient civilisations should feel genuinely old.
2. Ruins should have believable historical layers.
3. No civilisation should be completely explained at launch.
4. Ancient cultures should have distinct architecture and symbolism.
5. Ancient technology should not automatically be superior to modern technology.
6. Archaeological discoveries should create new questions.
7. Myths and historical records may contradict each other.
8. Players should be able to identify recurring civilisational symbols.
9. Ancient ruins should contain environmental storytelling.
10. Some ancient mysteries should remain unresolved.
11. Future expansions may reinterpret earlier discoveries.
12. New civilisations should connect naturally to existing history.
13. Ancient civilisation content should reward exploration.
14. The Ancient Archivarium should contain information that cannot be found elsewhere.
15. The Archivarium should not provide perfect or universally reliable answers.
16. Kaelgorath-related history should remain partially uncertain until later story content.
17. The existence of civilisations predating the Age of Dawn should remain a major unresolved mystery.
18. Ancient history should feel like something the player is discovering rather than something the game simply tells them.

---

# 39. Core Principle

Elysium is not a young world.

It is a world built on top of other worlds.

Cities were built over ruins.

Kingdoms were built over cities.

Modern roads follow ancient roads.

Religions preserve fragments of forgotten beliefs.

Languages contain words whose original meanings have been lost.

Weapons are forged using techniques nobody fully understands.

And beneath all of it are civilisations whose names have disappeared from history.

The player is not the first person to walk these lands.

They are simply one of the few who are willing to look closely enough to ask what was here before them.

Some answers will be found in books.

Some will be buried beneath ruins.

Some will be hidden inside the Ancient Archivarium.

And some may have been forgotten for a reason.
