## 1. Overview

Aggro (threat) determines which player an enemy prefers to attack. The system exists to give tanks a clear job and to make healer and DPS positioning matter, while remaining understandable for solo and pickup-group play.

---

## 2. Core Rules

- Threat is generated by damage dealt, healing done, and specific tank abilities.
- Tanks have tools (taunts, high-threat generators, defensive cooldowns) that let them reliably hold aggro against competent DPS.
- Healing generates threat, but at a lower coefficient than equivalent damage so that healers are not constantly pulling.
- Dropping threat (feign death, vanish, threat dumps) is available to certain classes as a defensive or utility tool.
- When a player dies or leaves combat, their threat is cleared; the AI re-evaluates remaining targets.

---

## 3. Tank Identity

Each tank specialisation has a distinct way of holding threat that reinforces its fantasy (e.g. steady sustained threat vs. burst threat windows). Exact coefficients and ability designs are tuned in [0309-Balance.md](../0300-Characters/0309-Balance.md) and the relevant talent trees.

---

## 4. Open-World vs Instance Behaviour

- In open world, social aggro (calling nearby allies) is used sparingly and telegraphed.
- In dungeons and raids, pull paths are designed so that accidental social aggro is minimised; intentional pack pulls remain a skill expression for tanks.

---

## 5. Technical Notes

Threat tables are maintained per enemy on the server. Clients receive only the current primary target information needed for the target frame and nameplate display. All threat modification is server-authoritative.
