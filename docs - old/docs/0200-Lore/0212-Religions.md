## 1. Overview

Religion in Elysium is defined by the silence of the gods. Since the Sundering, the Elysian Circle has not spoken directly to mortals. Faith therefore centres on legacy, ritual, and the hope (or fear) that the gods may one day return — or that mortals must now stand alone.

---

## 2. Major Faiths

### The Solar Path (Solthar)
Dominant among the Dawnbound Concord and humans of Aurelia. Emphasises order, light, rebuilding, and the restoration of the Age of Concord. The Sunspire Cathedral in Solmere is its greatest temple.

### The Night Vigil (Nyxara)
More common among Duskward-aligned spies, rogues, and those who believe the old order should stay buried. Values secrecy, self-reliance, and adaptation to a changed world.

### The Stone Covenant (Terravox)
Central to dwarven culture. Faith expressed through craftsmanship, endurance, and the keeping of ancestral holds. Ironpeak Hold’s deep halls contain the largest Terravox shrines.

### The Forge Flame (Ignareth)
Shared (with different emphasis) by both orc clan-fires and dwarven forges. One of the few gods still openly honoured across faction lines.

### The Green Communion (Aeloria)
Strong among druids, rangers, and rural communities. Her silence since the Sundering is a recurring source of grief and quest motivation, especially in forested regions.

### The Quiet Road (Voranthe)
Associated with death, fate, and the Revenant race. Not a death cult; rather a recognition that endings and transitions are part of the world’s cycle. Viewed with suspicion by many Concord citizens.

### The Storm Call (Maelithir)
Coastal and sailor faith. Storms are both danger and message; Maelith’s Herald world boss is seen by some as a divine remnant.

---

## 3. Design Rules

- Religion colours NPC dialogue, architecture, festivals, and seasonal events, but never gates core progression.
- Both Concord and Pact characters can hold personal faith that differs from their faction’s dominant tendency.
- Future expansions may introduce new cults or rediscovered rites without invalidating existing faiths.

---

## 4. Player-Facing Expression

- Shrines and temples act as flavour landmarks and occasional minor quest hubs.
- Seasonal festivals tied to specific gods appear in [0807-Seasons.md](../0800-Multiplayer/0807-Seasons.md).
- Certain class fantasy (Paladin, Cleric, Druid, Necromancer) draws directly from these traditions.
